Goals

Students should be able to:

  • describe the change of position of objects in terms of distance and displacement

  • present information on displacement-time graphs for moving objects

  • distinguish between scalar and vector quantities

  • use scalars and vectors to represent physical quantities

Quick check

Which arrow is the displacement for this trip?

Distance is the path: \( 500 + 500 = 1000\ \text{m} \). Displacement is the straight arrow from A to C: \( |\Delta\vec s| = \sqrt{500^2+500^2} = 707\ \text{m} \) at \( 45^\circ \) north of east. Average speed \( = 1000/625 = 1.6\ \text{m s}^{-1} \); average velocity \( = 707/625 = 1.13\ \text{m s}^{-1} \) NE.

BScalars and vectors

More

Left: adding vectors tip-to-tail — b starts where a ends, and the resultant a + b runs from the shared start to the final tip; the order does not matter. Right: O and P are 20 m apart; Peter walks out and straight back at a steady speed, the same time each way. Each arc is 62.8 m, so the whole trip is about 126 m of distance, yet he finishes where he started: displacement is zero, and so is his average velocity.

Scalar
Magnitude only — no direction.
  • distance
  • speed
  • mass
  • energy
  • time
Vector
Magnitude and direction — drawn as an arrow.
  • displacement
  • velocity
  • acceleration
  • force

Quick check

For the out-and-back trip: which quantity is zero?

He finishes at the start, so \( \Delta\vec s = \vec s_O - \vec s_O = 0 \) and the average velocity is zero too. Distance is not zero: \( 2 \times 62.8 = 126\ \text{m} \), so average speed \( = 126/t > 0 \). Same distance, opposite displacements on the two legs.

QDSE practice

2015 MC Q4

A particle travels at 2.0 m s⁻¹ due east for 1.5 s and then at 4.0 m s⁻¹ due north for 1.0 s. Find the magnitude of its average velocity for the whole journey.

The displacements are 3.0 m east and 4.0 m north, so the resultant displacement is √(3.0² + 4.0²) = 5.0 m. Average velocity = 5.0 m / 2.5 s = 2.0 m s⁻¹.
