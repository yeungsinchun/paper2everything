Uniform circular motion

ω, v²/r and centripetal force.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • define angular velocity as rate of change of angular displacement and relate to linear \( v = \omega r \)

  • state centripetal acceleration \( a = v^2/r = \omega^2 r \) and apply to uniform circular motion

  • realise resultant force points towards centre of uniform circular motion

AAngular velocity and \( a=v^2/r \)

Uniform circular motion

\[ \omega = \frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t} \qquad v = \omega r \qquad a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r \quad (\text{toward centre}) \] “Uniform” means speed uniform — velocity and acceleration are not.

BCentripetal force — what provides it

Concept check

Why is “centrifugal force” not a real force on the bung in the lab frame?

In the inertial frame the only horizontal force on the bung is tension toward the centre — that is the net force that causes the centripetal acceleration. “Centrifugal” is the sensation in the rotating frame, not a new force from another body.

CG-value and the washing machine

Concept check

Doubling speed at same radius quadruples the required centripetal force.

True — \( F=mv^2/r \).

Centripetal force is a new type of force like weight.

False — it is the role played by tension/gravity/normal etc., not a new kind.

2016 MC Q13 · Section 11 Uniform Circular Motion

Two points are fixed on one uniformly rotating platform at radii r and r/2. What is the ratio of their centripetal accelerations?

They share angular speed ω, and a = ω²r. The ratio is 2:1.
