Book 2 · Ch.4

Newton’s second and third laws

F=ma, free-body and action–reaction. Net force causes acceleration, and every force has a partner on another body — the free-body diagram is the bookkeeping that makes Newton II and III useful.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • describe effect of a net force on speed and/or direction

  • state Newton’s Second Law \( F = ma \), verify experimentally, use newton as unit

  • use free-body diagrams, determine net force, apply \( F = ma \) in one dimension

  • realise forces act in pairs, state Newton’s Third Law and identify action–reaction

  • distinguish between mass and weight, realise \( W = mg \)

ANewton II — \( F_{\text{net}} = ma \) and the free-body

Free-body

Draw one object, mark all forces on it (weight \( mg \) down, normal \( N \), tension \( T \), friction \( f \)). Then \( \sum F_x = ma_x,\ \sum F_y = ma_y \). Coupled bodies share the same \( a \) along the string (inextensible, light).

StepWhat to do
1Isolate the body
2List forces with direction
3Write \( F_{\text{net}} = ma \) per direction

Concept check

Student varies pull on a constant-mass trolley on a horizontal table. What should the \( a\!-\!F \) graph look like if friction is blocked out?

B — \( a = F/m \); slope \( 1/m \) if compensated; with friction the line intercepts \( F \).

BNewton III and mass vs weight

Mass vs weight

Mass \( m \) (kg) — amount of matter, same everywhere. Weight \( W = mg \) (N) — gravitational force on that mass; \( g \) changes (e.g. Moon \( \sim 1.62\ \text{m s}^{-2} \)). Weighing measures \( N \) which equals \( mg \) at rest.

Concept check

Weight and normal force on a block at rest on a table are an action–reaction pair.

False — same body. Pairs are across two bodies (Earth–block for weight).

Static friction can be larger than \( \mu_s N \).

False — \( f_s \le \mu_s N \); kinetic ~ \( \mu_k N \).

CVerifying \( F=ma \)

Concept check

Doubling the pull with same trolley doubles the acceleration (friction compensated).

True — \( a=F/m \).

Mass and weight are the same thing.

False — mass is matter (kg), weight is force (N) \( mg \).

DInclined plane

Concept check

Block released on rough incline slides down. \( a \)?

A — net down-slope \( mg\sin\theta - f \), \( f=\mu N =\mu mg\cos\theta \).
