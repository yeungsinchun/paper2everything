Moment, equilibrium and centre of gravity

Torque, balance and C.G.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • define moment of a force as product of force and perpendicular distance from pivot

  • discuss uses of torques and couples

  • state conditions for equilibrium of forces on a rigid body and solve about a fixed pivot

  • interpret centre of gravity and determine it experimentally

AMoment — force times perpendicular distance

Moment / torque

\( M = F d_\perp \) clockwise vs anticlockwise. A couple is two equal opposite forces separated — pure rotation. Perpendicular distance matters, not the slant length.

Concept check

Seesaw pivot O, PQ = QO = OR = RS. Balanced. Swap P↔Q and R↔S, still balanced. What must hold?

A — swap symmetry requires pairwise equality to keep \( \sum M=0 \).

BEquilibrium and centre of gravity

Equilibrium

Rigid body: (1) \( \sum \vec F=0 \) (no translation) and (2) \( \sum M=0 \) (no rotation). For a regular shape with uniform density, C.G. is geometric centre; hanging gives two lines that cross at C.G.

Concept check

How do you find the C.G. of an irregular lamina experimentally?

Hang from two different points; hang a plumb line each time and draw the vertical through the suspension. The intersection is C.G. (weight acts there).

CLevers and couples

Concept check

Door handle far from hinges — why?

C — moment grows with distance, so same push turns more.

DFinding C.G. — plumb line

Concept check

Why two hangs, not one?

One hang gives a line; the C.G. can be anywhere on that line. Two lines pin it.

2012 MC Q6 · Section 07 Force and Motion

A uniform 2 m gangplank is hinged at one end and raised by a force at the other. Where does its weight act when taking moments about the hinge?

Its weight acts at its midpoint, 1 m from the hinge. In equilibrium, clockwise and anticlockwise moments about the hinge are equal.
