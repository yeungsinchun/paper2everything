Book 2 · Ch.2

Velocity, acceleration and uniformly accelerated motion

Speed, velocity, acceleration and UAM. Velocity and acceleration are rates of change; their graphs and the four UAM equations let you solve any straight-line motion with constant acceleration, including free-fall.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • define average speed as distance travelled in a given period of time and average velocity as displacement changed in a period of time

  • distinguish between instantaneous and average speed/velocity

  • present information on velocity-time graphs and acceleration-time graphs

  • derive equations of uniformly accelerated motion \( v = u + at \), \( s = \tfrac12(u+v)t \), \( s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2 \), \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as \) and solve problems

  • examine free-fall and estimate \( g \), describe effect of air resistance

ASpeed and velocity · instantaneous vs average

Definitions

Average speed \( = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\Delta t} \) (scalar). Average velocity \( = \dfrac{\Delta\vec s}{\Delta t} \) (vector). Instantaneous = limit \( \Delta t \to 0 \) — the tangent.

Concept check

A car holds \( 15\ \text{m s}^{-1} \) round a bend radius \( 50\ \text{m} \). What changes while speed stays the same?

Speed scalar, velocity vector — turning changes velocity even at constant speed. Displacement is the chord, smaller than arc length.

BUniformly accelerated motion — the four equations

UAM equations (a constant)

\[ v = u + at \qquad s = \tfrac12(u+v)t \qquad s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2 \qquad v^2 = u^2 + 2as \]

Choose the one that has your unknown and not the one you don’t need. Signs carry direction: take one direction as positive.

If you knowUse
\( u, a, t \) → \( v \)\( v=u+at \)
\( u, v, t \) → \( s \)\( s=\tfrac12(u+v)t \)
\( u, a, t \) → \( s \)\( s=ut+\tfrac12 at^2 \)
\( u, v, a \) → \( s \) (no \( t \))\( v^2=u^2+2as \)

Concept check

A ball falls \( 3\ \text{m s}^{-1} \) down, bounces to \( 2\ \text{m s}^{-1} \) up in \( 0.2\ \text{s} \). Average acceleration (take up as +)?

\( a = (v-u)/t = (2 - (-3))/0.2 = 25\ \text{m s}^{-2} \) up. Sign matters — take one direction as positive and keep it.

CFree-fall and \( g \)

Free-fall

Vertical UAM with \( a = g \) downward if air negligible. Light gates or ticker give \( v\!-\!t \); slope \( = g \). The resident’s jump (2.5 s to cushion, then 3 m thickness) and the basketball hang-time are solved with the same four equations, using \( s = ut + \tfrac12 gt^2 \) with sign for up/down.

Concept check

Why does the “hang-time” of a good jumper feel long? Use \( g \).

Vertical motion is symmetric: time up = time down. High jump means high \( u \); \( t_{\text{flight}} = 2u/g \), so big \( u \) gives long air time plus perception at the top where vertical speed is near zero.

Concept check

In uniformly accelerated motion, \( v\!-\!t \) is a straight line.

True — constant slope = constant \( a \).

Ticker spacing increasing uniformly means constant acceleration.

True — distance per time interval grows linearly with \( v \).

DMeasuring \( g \)

Concept check

A student gets \( g=10.2\ \text{m s}^{-2} \). What went wrong?

Air drag not corrected, or timing includes reaction. The accepted is 9.81; close is fine — method must show slope of \( v\!-\!t \), not just one drop.
