Projectile motion

Parabolic trajectory — horizontal and vertical.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • describe shape of path of projectile launched at an angle

  • understand independence of horizontal and vertical motions

  • solve problems involving projectile motion

APath and the two directions

Independence

\[ x = (u\cos\theta) t \qquad y = (u\sin\theta) t - \tfrac12 gt^2 \qquad v_x = u\cos\theta,\; v_y = u\sin\theta - gt \] Only weight acts (vertical). Horizontal and vertical are independent but share \( t \).

Concept check

Baseball thrown 40° above horizontal, horizontal component 12 m s\(^{-1}\). Initial speed?

\( v_x = v\cos40^\circ =12 \) → \( v =12/\cos40≈15.7 \) m s\(^{-1}\).

BBomber and the horizontal launch

Concept check

A ball launched horizontally at 0.5 m s\(^{-1}\) from 6 m above ground. How does \( v_x \) affect time of flight?

Not at all — vertical \( t = \sqrt{2h/g} \) uses only height and \( g \). Horizontal determines range \( x=v_x t \).

CRange, time of flight and the optimal angle

Concept check

Why does the Javelin RQ mention air and rule changes?

Real javelin has drag/lift; shape matters. Rules shifted to reduce range by moving C.G. Experimentally the \( 45^\circ \) ideal is only for vacuum point mass.

2012 MC Q12 · Section 10 Projectile Motion

A bomber flies horizontally at 200 m s⁻¹, 1 km above ground. Neglecting air resistance, how far before a target should it release a bomb? Use g = 9.8 m s⁻².

Time to fall is √(2h/g) ≈ 14.3 s; horizontal distance is vt ≈ 2.86 km.
