Gas Laws and Kinetic Theory · Summary

Select a gas relation by checking what stays fixed. Use Kelvin temperatures.

ADefinitions

Pressure
Normal force per unit area; for a gas, caused by molecular collisions on container walls.
Absolute temperature
Temperature in kelvins, measured from absolute zero.
Ideal gas
A model gas with negligible molecular size and intermolecular forces.
Atmospheric pressure
Pressure due to air; standard value near sea level is about 100 kPa.

BChoose the law

Fixed condition Relation Paired states
Temperature and gas amount Boyle: \(pV=\text{constant}\) \(p_1V_1=p_2V_2\)
Volume and gas amount Pressure: \(p/T=\text{constant}\) \(p_1/T_1=p_2/T_2\)
Pressure and gas amount Charles: \(V/T=\text{constant}\) \(V_1/T_1=V_2/T_2\)
\[\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}\qquad pV=nRT\]

Convert Celsius to kelvins: \(T(\mathrm K)=\theta(^{\circ}\mathrm C)+273\). Use SI units with \(R=8.31\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\).

CKinetic explanation

  • Pressure comes from molecule-wall collisions and momentum transfer.
  • At fixed volume, heating raises speed and collision rate, increasing pressure.
  • At fixed pressure, heating causes expansion so collision rate per area stays balanced.
  • At fixed temperature, compression increases collision frequency and pressure.

DCommon traps

  • Check fixed conditions and constant gas amount before applying a law.
  • Use kelvins in all temperature ratios.
  • Pressure acts in all directions; force from pressure acts perpendicular to a surface.
  • Do not confuse molecular speed with average kinetic energy: at the same temperature, lighter molecules move faster on average.
  • Ideal-gas assumptions work best at high temperature and low pressure.
