Quick check

At the same gas volume, heating increases pressure. Which molecular change explains it?

B. Higher temperature means greater average kinetic energy and stronger, more frequent wall collisions.

BUse models carefully

A vibrating platform can make beads bounce inside a cylinder beneath a movable piston. The beads represent gas molecules; platform drive represents heating; piston height represents gas volume; piston load represents outside pressure.

Change in model Gas idea
Drive the platform harder Raise temperature: particles move faster.
Add beads Increase the amount of gas and collision frequency.
Add weight to piston Raise outside pressure and compress the gas.

The model makes collisions visible but beads need continuous input from the platform. Real gas molecules move between collisions without a vibrating floor. Beads also lose energy and are much larger relative to their container.

Explain the limit

Why does the bead model need a vibrating platform while a gas does not?

The platform continually supplies energy to the beads. Gas molecules already have kinetic energy and move freely between collisions.

CIdeal gases are a useful model

Ideal gas

An ideal gas is a model whose molecules have negligible size, move randomly, and exert negligible forces on each other except during elastic collisions.

Real gases behave more like the ideal model at high temperature and low pressure. Molecules are farther apart and intermolecular effects matter less. At low temperature or high pressure, real gases can depart from ideal behaviour or condense.

Quick check

When is a real gas most likely to behave ideally?

D. Molecules are farther apart and move freely, so their size and interactions matter less.

DQuantitative kinetic theory (extension)

For an ideal gas, the kinetic-theory equation connects pressure and volume to the number, mass and motion of molecules:

\[pV=\frac{1}{3}Nm\langle v^2\rangle\]

\(N\): number of molecules; \(m\): mass per molecule; \(\langle v^2\rangle\): mean square speed. The root-mean-square speed is \(v_{rms}=\sqrt{\langle v^2\rangle}\).

Combining this model with the ideal gas law shows that average translational kinetic energy per molecule is proportional to absolute temperature. This advanced relation explains why Kelvin temperature is fundamental to gas laws.

Quick check

At the same Kelvin temperature, two ideal gases have the same average translational kinetic energy per molecule. Which quantity may differ?

A. For equal kinetic energy, lighter molecules move faster than heavier molecules.
