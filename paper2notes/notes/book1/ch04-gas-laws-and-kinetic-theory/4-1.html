Gas pressure

A gas pushes on its container because its moving molecules collide with the walls.

APressure is force spread over area

Pressure

Pressure is the component of force perpendicular to a surface divided by the area it acts on.

\[p=\frac{F_\perp}{A}\]

\(p\): pressure in pascals (Pa); \(F_\perp\): normal force in newtons; \(A\): area in square metres. \(1\,\mathrm{Pa}=1\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}\).

Worked example · contact area

  1. A 600 N load acts over \(0.030\,\mathrm{m^2}\).
  2. Use \(p=F/A=600/0.030\).
  3. Pressure = 20 kPa.

Quick check

The same force acts on a smaller area. What happens to the pressure?

A. With force fixed, reducing area increases force per unit area.

BMolecular collisions make gas pressure

Each impact changes a molecule's momentum and exerts an opposite force on the wall. Enormous numbers of impacts produce a steady average pressure. Faster molecules hit harder and more often; a greater number density also raises collision frequency.

  • Heating a gas increases average molecular speed.
  • Compressing a fixed amount of gas crowds molecules into less volume.
  • Adding gas molecules increases collision frequency.

Quick check

A sealed rigid container is heated. Why does its gas pressure rise?

C. Higher temperature means greater average molecular speed, increasing impact and collision rate.

CAtmospheric pressure

Air molecules collide with objects around us. At sea level, standard atmospheric pressure is about \(100\,\mathrm{kPa}\). Pressure generally falls at higher altitude as the air becomes less dense.

Quick check

A straw contains lower pressure than the drink outside it. What pushes liquid up the straw?

D. Pressure differences produce the net push. The liquid is not pulled upward by a vacuum.

DUse force balance on a piston

A stationary piston has zero resultant force. Gas pressure, outside pressure and piston weight must balance. Compare forces on the piston, not pressures alone.

\[p_{in}A=p_{out}A+W\]

For a freely moving piston with negligible friction, this relation applies in equilibrium. Divide each force by the same piston area to obtain the pressure relation.
