Gas laws

The gas laws relate pressure, volume and temperature when the amount of gas stays fixed.

Remember

Fixed temperature\(pV=\text{constant}\)

Fixed volume\(p/T=\text{constant}\)

Fixed pressure\(V/T=\text{constant}\)

Temperatureuse kelvins: \(T=\theta+273\)

ABoyleBPressureCCharlesDIdeal gas

ABoyle's law: fixed temperature

Boyle's law

For a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature, pressure varies inversely with volume.

\[p_1V_1=p_2V_2\]

Keep volume units consistent on both sides. If the volume halves, pressure doubles, provided no gas enters or leaves and temperature remains fixed.

Quick check

A fixed gas sample is compressed to half its volume at constant temperature. Its pressure:

B. The product \(pV\) stays constant.

BPressure law: fixed volume

Pressure law

For a fixed amount of gas at constant volume, pressure is proportional to absolute temperature.

\[\frac{p_1}{T_1}=\frac{p_2}{T_2}\]

Use kelvins, not degrees Celsius. Heating raises average molecular speed and makes wall collisions more frequent and forceful.

Worked example · warming a rigid canister

  1. Initial state: 100 kPa at 27 °C = 300 K.
  2. Final temperature: 87 °C = 360 K.
  3. At fixed volume, \(p_2=100\times360/300\).
  4. Final pressure = 120 kPa.

Quick check

Why must a gas-law calculation use Kelvin temperature?

C. The direct proportionality uses absolute temperature; Celsius has an offset.

CCharles' law: fixed pressure

Charles' law

For a fixed amount of gas at constant pressure, volume is proportional to absolute temperature.

\[\frac{V_1}{T_1}=\frac{V_2}{T_2}\]

Heating makes molecules move faster. The gas expands until the collision rate per area balances the outside pressure.

Quick check

A flexible gas container stays at constant pressure and cools from 300 K to 240 K. How does its volume change?

A. At fixed pressure, \(V_2/V_1=T_2/T_1=240/300=0.8\).

DCombine the gas laws

For a fixed amount of gas, the three relations combine into one paired-state rule:

\[\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}\]

For an ideal gas, pressure, volume, amount in moles and Kelvin temperature obey:

\[pV=nRT\]

\(n\): amount in moles; \(R=8.31\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\); use compatible SI units. A mole contains about \(6.02\times10^{23}\) particles.

First list what is fixed. Then choose a relation and convert every temperature to kelvins.

Choose a law

A sealed rigid container is heated. Which quantities are fixed, and which gas law links the changing pressure and temperature?

The amount of gas and volume are fixed. Use the pressure law, \(p/T=\text{constant}\), with Kelvin temperatures.
