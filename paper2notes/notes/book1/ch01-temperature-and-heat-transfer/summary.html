Chapter 1

Summary: Temperature and Heat Transfer

Formula, definitions, the three transfer processes side by side, and the usual traps.

Goals

Students should be able to:

  • realise temperature as the degree of hotness of an object

  • interpret temperature as a quantity associated with the average kinetic energy due to the random motion of molecules in a system

  • explain the use of temperature-dependent properties in measuring temperature

  • define and use degree Celsius as a unit of temperature

  • realise that heat is the energy transferred as a result of the temperature difference between two objects

  • describe the effect of mass, temperature and state of matter on the internal energy of a system

  • relate internal energy to the sum of the kinetic energy of random motion and the potential energy of molecules in the system

  • identify the means of energy transfer in terms of conduction, convection and radiation

  • interpret energy transfer by conduction in terms of molecular motion

  • realise the emission of infra-red radiation by hot objects

  • determine the factors affecting the emission and absorption of radiation

4Traps that cost marks

Average, not total

Temperature measures the average KE per molecule. A bigger mass at the same temperature has more total KE and more internal energy.

Temperature sets the direction

Heat flows from higher to lower temperature, even into a body with more internal energy. It stops at equal temperatures.

Bodies hold internal energy

Write "internal energy" for what a body contains and "heat" only for energy on the move.

No "cold" flows

A metal feels cold because heat leaves your hand quickly, not because cold enters it.

Offset the zero

In proportion problems subtract the ice-point reading first; a column is not zero length at 0 °C.

Vacuum vs radiation

The vacuum in a flask stops conduction and convection. Only the silvering cuts radiation.

Absorber = emitter

Dull black absorbs and emits well; shiny absorbs and emits poorly and reflects well.

Convection needs gravity and a fluid

No convection in solids, and none in a weightless spacecraft.
