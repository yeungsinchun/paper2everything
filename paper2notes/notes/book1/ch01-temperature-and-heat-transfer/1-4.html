Goals

Students should be able to:

  • identify the means of energy transfer in terms of conduction, convection and radiation

  • determine the factors affecting the emission and absorption of radiation

AVacuum flask: block every path

FeatureReducesHow
Stopper (cork or plastic)convection, evaporation, conduction at the topstops warm air leaving and outside air mixing in; plastic and cork conduct poorly
Vacuum between double wallsconduction and convectionboth need particles; the gap has almost none
Silvered wall surfacesradiationpoor emitter on one side, good reflector on the other
Insulating supportconductionsmall contact through a poor conductor
  • A flask slows heat flow in both directions. It keeps cold drinks cold for the same reasons it keeps soup hot; it does not "know" which is needed.
  • Some heat still leaks, mostly through the stopper and the neck, where the glass walls join.

Quick check

Iced water is stored in a vacuum flask on a hot day. Which statement is correct?

C. Heat flows in from the warmer surroundings, and the flask slows it. A: a flask supplies no cooling. B: radiation crosses a vacuum; the vacuum stops conduction and convection. D: it slows heat flow either way.

BHeat sink: open every path

FeatureSpeeds up
Good contact with a metal blockconduction from the chip into the sink
Many thin finsloss to the air: much larger surface area
Fanforced convection: carries warm air away and brings cool air in
Dull black finish (car radiators, fridge coils)radiation: good emitter

Quick check

Why does a heat sink have many thin fins instead of one solid block of the same mass?

B. Heat leaves through the surface, so more area means a faster loss. C: the material decides conduction, not its thickness. D would keep heat in, the opposite of the aim; the gaps are open for air to flow through.

CGreenhouses and the greenhouse effect

  • Glass lets visible light through but is fairly opaque to the long-wavelength infrared that the warm soil and plants give out.
  • The closed glass also stops the warm air being replaced by cooler outside air. Blocking this convection and ventilation is often the larger effect.
  • A car parked in the sun is a small greenhouse; shiny sunshields reflect sunlight before it can be absorbed inside.
Greenhouse effect

In the greenhouse effect, gases in the atmosphere such as carbon dioxide and water vapour let most sunlight reach the ground but absorb and re-emit part of the infrared the warm Earth gives out, keeping the Earth warmer. Extra carbon dioxide from burning fuel strengthens it (global warming).

Quick check

On a sunny day the inside of a parked car with closed windows becomes much hotter than the air outside. Which explanation is best?

A is the greenhouse mechanism. B has the energy entering the wrong way. C cannot keep a car hot for hours. D: air insulating would not make the inside hotter than outside; energy must come in, as sunlight.
