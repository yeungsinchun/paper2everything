Section 1.3

Ways of heat transfer

Convection moves the fluid, conduction passes energy along, radiation needs nothing in between.

Remember

Convectionwarm fluid rises, cool fluid sinks: the fluid itself carries the energy

Conductioncollisions pass energy particle to particle; free electrons make metals fast

Radiationinfrared waves; no medium needed; crosses a vacuum

Surfacesdull black: good absorber and emitter · shiny: good reflector

Goals

Students should be able to:

  • identify the means of energy transfer in terms of conduction, convection and radiation

  • interpret energy transfer by conduction in terms of molecular motion

  • realise the emission of infra-red radiation by hot objects

  • determine the factors affecting the emission and absorption of radiation

AConvection: the fluid moves

Convection

Convection is heat transfer by the flow of a fluid (liquid or gas). Heated fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place, setting up a convection current.

  • Only fluids convect: the particles must be free to flow. Solids cannot.
  • It depends on gravity: less dense fluid rises only because denser fluid is pulled down. In orbit a flame is round.
  • Heat from the bottom (kettle element), cool from the top (freezer coil, air conditioner high on a wall).
  • A fan or pump moving the fluid is forced convection; it is not caused by density differences.

Quick check

Why is the heating element of an electric kettle placed at the bottom?

B. A is false: water is a poor conductor; heated at the top, only the top would get hot. C mixes up heat with hot fluid: warm fluid rises, but conduction goes in every direction. D is not the reason; radiation is small here.

BConduction: passed on by collisions

Conduction

Conduction is heat transfer through a material by collisions between its particles: faster-vibrating particles in the hot part pass energy to slower neighbours, layer by layer. In metals, free electrons also carry energy quickly through the material.

Good conductorsPoor conductors (insulators)
Metals: silver, copper, aluminium, steel (many free electrons)Glass, wood, plastic, water, and above all air
Used to move heat: pans, heat sinks, skewersMaterials with trapped air (wool, feathers, foam, glass wool) insulate best
  • Conduction happens in solids, liquids and gases, but liquids and gases conduct poorly; in fluids, convection does most of the work.
  • Metal at room temperature feels colder than wood at the same temperature: the metal conducts heat away from your hand faster. Your hand senses the rate of heat loss, not the temperature.

Quick check

A metal tray and a wooden board have been in the same room all night. The tray feels colder to touch because

C. Both are at room temperature (A is false). There is no such thing as "cold" flowing (B): heat flows from your warmer hand into the tray. D is irrelevant; the rate of heat flow decides the feeling.

CRadiation: infrared needs no medium

Radiation

Radiation is heat transfer by electromagnetic waves, mainly infrared. It needs no medium, so it is the only way heat crosses a vacuum (Sun to Earth).

  • Every object emits infrared, hot or cold. A hotter object emits at a higher rate, and when hot enough it also glows (red from about 500 °C).
  • Every object also absorbs radiation. Hotter than the surroundings → it emits more than it absorbs (net emitter, it cools). Colder → net absorber, it warms.
  • An infrared thermometer reads temperature from the infrared a surface gives out, without touching it.
SurfaceAbsorberEmitterReflectorUses
Dull blackgoodgoodpoorsolar water heaters, cooling fins and radiators
Shiny, light-colouredpoorpoorgoodsurvival blankets, flask linings, sunshields
Exam tip

Good absorbers are good emitters. A black road gets hotter by day and also cools faster at night.

Quick check

A shiny foil blanket is wrapped around a runner after a race. It keeps the runner warm mainly because

D. Shiny surfaces are poor emitters and good reflectors. A and C give the shiny surface black-surface properties. B: a blanket supplies no energy; it only slows the loss (it also traps a layer of still air).

Quick check

(a) A block of ice gives out infrared radiation.

True. Every object emits infrared. Ice in a warm room absorbs more than it emits, so it is a net absorber.

(b) Radiation cannot pass through a vacuum because there are no particles to carry it.

False. Radiation is electromagnetic waves and needs no particles; conduction and convection need a medium.
