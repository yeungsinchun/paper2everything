APower: energy per second

Power

Power is the rate of energy transfer: the energy transferred per unit time. Its SI unit is the watt (W); 1 W = 1 J s⁻¹.

\[ P = \frac{E}{t} \qquad E = Pt \]

\(P\): power (W) · \(E\): energy transferred (J) · \(t\): time (s). Convert minutes and hours to seconds first.

  • At constant power, \(E \propto t\): double the time, double the energy. The E–t graph is a straight line through the origin, and its slope is the power.
  • If the power changes, the energy is the area under the P–t graph.
  • Prefixes: 1 kJ = 10³ J, 1 MJ = 10⁶ J, 1 kW = 10³ W, 1 MW = 10⁶ W.

Quick check

Which of these is not a unit of power?

C. The kilojoule is a unit of energy. Power must be energy per time, so it is J s⁻¹ (= W), or a multiple such as MW.

Worked example Two kettles

Kettle X is rated 1.2 kW and kettle Y 2.0 kW. Each holds the same mass of water at the same starting temperature. (a) How much energy does each supply in 4 minutes? (b) Which boils first?

  1. \(t = 4 \times 60 = 240\ \mathrm{s}\).
  2. X: \(E = Pt = 1200 \times 240 = 2.88 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{J}\). Y: \(E = 2000 \times 240 = 4.80 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{J}\).
  3. Both need the same energy to boil the water; Y supplies it faster.

(a) 288 kJ and 480 kJ · (b) Y

BUseful power and losses

Not all the energy a device takes in heats the thing you want. The rest heats the container and the surroundings.

\[ \text{useful energy} = \text{efficiency} \times P \times t \]
Exam tip

Energy supplied = energy gained by the body + energy lost. In a "find the time" question, losses make the real time longer.

Worked example Solar water heater

Sunlight delivers 1.0 kW on each square metre. A 1.5 m² panel passes 50% of what it receives to the water. How long does it take to deliver 3.0 MJ to the water?

  1. Useful power \(= 0.50 \times 1000 \times 1.5 = 750\ \mathrm{W}\).
  2. \(t = E/P = 3.0 \times 10^{6} / 750 = 4000\ \mathrm{s}\).

4000 s ≈ 67 min

Quick check

An oven takes in 1500 W and loses 60% of it to the surroundings. How much energy heats the food in 10 minutes?

B. 40% is useful: \(0.40 \times 1500 \times 600 = 3.6 \times 10^{5}\) J. A uses the 60% that is lost. C is the total energy taken in. D forgets to change 10 minutes into 600 seconds.
