Chapter 2

Summary: Heat Capacity

Formula sheet, definitions, a values table to recognise, and the usual traps.

Goals

Students should be able to:

  • define heat capacity as \( C = \dfrac{Q}{\Delta T} \) and specific heat capacity as \( c = \dfrac{Q}{m\Delta T} \)

  • determine the specific heat capacity of a substance

  • discuss the practical importance of the high specific heat capacity of water

  • solve problems involving heat capacity and specific heat capacity

1Formula sheet

\[ P = \frac{E}{t} \qquad E = Pt \]
\[ C = \frac{E}{\Delta T} \qquad c = \frac{E}{m\,\Delta T} \qquad C = mc \]
\[ E = mc\,\Delta T \]
\[ \frac{\Delta T}{t} = \frac{P}{mc} \quad \text{(no heat loss)} \]
\[ m_1c_1(T_1 - T) = m_2c_2(T - T_2) \quad \text{(released = absorbed)} \]

2Definitions

TermMeaning
Power \(P\)energy transferred per second; unit W = J s⁻¹
Heat capacity \(C\)energy needed to raise the temperature of a body by 1 °C; unit J °C⁻¹; depends on mass and material
Specific heat capacity \(c\)energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a material by 1 °C; unit J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹; a property of the material
Final (mixture) temperaturethe common temperature hot and cold bodies reach in thermal equilibrium
Method of mixturesfinding \(c\) of a solid by putting it, hot, into water and using energy released = energy absorbed

4Traps that cost marks

Seconds, kilograms, joules

Convert minutes to seconds, grams to kilograms and kW to W before you use \(E = Pt\) or \(E = mc\Delta T\).

\(C\) belongs to a body

Heat capacity grows with mass. Specific heat capacity is fixed for a material, whatever the mass.

Heat loss pushes \(c\) up

In the electrical method some energy escapes, so the measured rise is too small and the calculated \(c\) is too large.

Steeper is not heavier

With the same heater, the steepest heating line belongs to the body with the smallest \(mc\).

Differences, not readings

Use \(T_1 - T\) for the hot body and \(T - T_2\) for the cold one. Both are positive.

Count every cold body

When the container also warms (cup, teapot, calorimeter), add its \(mc\Delta T\) to the energy absorbed.

Final T lies between

The mixture temperature is always between the two starting values, and closer to the body with the larger \(mc\).

High \(c\) means slow change

Water warms and cools slowly. That is why coastal places have a small daily temperature range.
