ACT slices and reconstruction

In CT, an X-ray tube and detector rotate around the patient. Each view records transmitted intensity from many paths. A computer combines the projections to estimate attenuation in small volume elements; image brightness represents the reconstructed attenuation map.

Definition

A CT image is a cross-sectional map of X-ray attenuation, not a photograph of reflected light.

Back-projection idea

Why many angles?

  1. One projection shows where attenuation occurred along each ray, but not its exact depth.
  2. Repeat from different angles and spread each measurement back along its ray.
  3. Overlapping contributions locate the attenuating structures.

Quick check

What does the brightness of a reconstructed CT pixel represent?

CT reconstruction estimates attenuation in each voxel; display brightness encodes that estimate.

CT helps separate structures that overlap in a projection. That added information comes with trade-offs, including greater dose in many examinations, cost and scan time.

Quick check

Why can CT distinguish structures that overlap on a plain radiograph?

The many projections support reconstruction of a cross-section rather than one superimposed shadow.
