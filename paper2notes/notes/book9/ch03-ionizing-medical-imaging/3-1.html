AIonizing radiation and X-rays

Ionizing radiation has enough energy to remove electrons from atoms or molecules. X-rays are high-frequency electromagnetic waves. An X-ray tube accelerates electrons into a heavy metal target; rapid deceleration produces X-rays.

ChangeEffect on X-ray attenuation
Greater densitymore attenuation
Higher atomic numbermore attenuation
Lower photon energymore attenuation

Quick check

How are X-rays produced in a conventional tube?

Fast electrons lose kinetic energy when they strike the target; some energy leaves as X-ray photons.

BAttenuation and half-value thickness

\[I=I_0e^{-\mu x},\qquad x_{1/2}=\frac{\ln2}{\mu}\]

The linear attenuation coefficient \(\mu\) depends on the material and photon energy. A half-value thickness (HVT) reduces intensity to one-half. After \(n\) HVTs, the remaining fraction is \((1/2)^n\).

Worked example

Three half-value thicknesses

  1. After one HVT: \(I/I_0=1/2\).
  2. After three HVTs: \(I/I_0=(1/2)^3=1/8\).
  3. 12.5% of the original intensity remains.

Quick check

A beam passes through material equal to four HVTs. What fraction remains?

Each HVT halves the beam: (1/2)⁴ = 1/16.

CRadiographic images

MaterialTypical radiograph appearanceReason
Airdarklittle attenuation
Soft tissuegreymoderate attenuation
Bonebrightgreater attenuation

A radiograph is a projection: the detector records X-rays that pass through the body. Dense bone attenuates more, so fewer X-rays reach that part of the detector. Contrast media can make some soft structures easier to distinguish.

Quick check

Why does bone look bright on a radiograph?

The detector darkens more where it receives more X-rays. Bone absorbs and scatters more, leaving a brighter image region.
