Radionuclide imaging

A radioactive tracer emits gamma rays from within the body, revealing its distribution and function.

ATracer and radionuclide choice

A radionuclide tracer combines a radioactive atom with a carrier that collects in a chosen organ or tissue. A suitable radionuclide emits penetrating gamma rays, has a useful half-life and can be attached to a suitable carrier.

ChoiceReason
Gamma emitterphotons can escape the body
Not alphaalpha particles are stopped within tissue
Useful half-lifeallows imaging without prolonged activity
Suitable chemistrycarrier directs the tracer to the target

Quick check

Why is a gamma emitter preferred for imaging from outside the body?

A detector outside the body must receive the emitted radiation; alpha particles are stopped too readily in tissue.

BEffective half-life

\[\frac1{T_e}=\frac1{T_p}+\frac1{T_b},\qquad A=A_0e^{-kt},\quad k=\frac{\ln2}{T_e}\]

Physical decay reduces the number of radioactive atoms. Biological processes remove tracer from the body. Since both reduce activity, the effective half-life is shorter than either individual half-life.

Quick check

Physical half-life is 6 h and biological half-life is 3 h. What is the effective half-life?

1/Tₑ = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 per hour, so Tₑ = 2 h.

Many detected events build an image of tracer distribution. A region with more tracer gives more counts (a hot spot); a region with less tracer gives fewer counts (a cold spot). Dynamic images show uptake or clearance over time.

Quick check

What does a hot spot usually indicate in a radionuclide image?

A hot spot is a region with more detected tracer activity; its clinical meaning depends on context.

DRadionuclide imaging compared with X-rays

Radionuclide imagingX-ray imaging
emission from a tracertransmission through the body

Emission images can reveal function, while radiographs and CT primarily map structure. Each method answers a different clinical question; no single method is best for every target.

Quick check

Which imaging method directly shows where an administered tracer accumulates?

Radionuclide imaging detects emissions from the tracer after it accumulates in tissue.
