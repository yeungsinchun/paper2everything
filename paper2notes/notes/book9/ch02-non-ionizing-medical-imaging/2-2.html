APiezoelectric transducer

Ultrasound is sound above 20 kHz. Medical scanners commonly use frequencies in the MHz range. In a piezoelectric crystal, squeezing or stretching produces a voltage; applying a changing voltage makes it vibrate and emit sound.

PartPurpose
Crystal and electrodesconvert between voltage and vibration
Backingdamps vibration to shorten the pulse
Acoustic windowpasses sound into tissue
Coupling gelremoves air gaps at the skin

Quick check

What happens when a piezoelectric crystal is compressed?

Mechanical strain separates charge in the crystal, creating a voltage.

BImpedance and reflection

Acoustic impedance is density times sound speed, \(Z=\rho c\). At a boundary, the intensity reflection fraction is \(\alpha=((Z_2-Z_1)/(Z_2+Z_1))^2\). The remainder transmits if absorption is ignored. Air and skin have a huge mismatch; gel fills the air gap and improves transmission.

\[Z=\rho c,\qquad \alpha=\frac{I_r}{I_0}=\left(\frac{Z_2-Z_1}{Z_2+Z_1}\right)^2\]

Quick check

Two media have the same acoustic impedance. What fraction reflects at their ideal boundary?

Equal impedances make the numerator zero, so the ideal reflection coefficient is zero.

CPulse-echo scans

The A-scan plots echo amplitude against time. A B-scan places echo brightness at the corresponding depth and beam direction to form a cross-section. A dark region can mean little reflection at its boundary; it does not automatically mean strong absorption.

\[s=\frac{ct}{2}\]
Worked example

Depth from echo time

  1. An echo returns after 50 μs in tissue where \(c=1600\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
  2. \(s=ct/2=1600(50 \times10^{-6})/2=0.040\,\mathrm m\).
  3. The boundary is 4.0 cm below the transducer.

Quick check

An echo returns after 20 μs in tissue with sound speed 1500 m/s. What is the boundary depth?

The sound travels out and back: depth = 1500 × 20×10⁻⁶ / 2 = 0.015 m.

DResolution and penetration

FrequencyWavelength / beamTrade-off
Highershorter wavelength; narrower beambetter detail, more attenuation
Lowerlonger wavelength; broader beamdeeper penetration, less detail

Axial resolution improves with shorter pulses. Lateral resolution improves with a narrower beam. Higher frequency can sharpen the image but is absorbed more strongly, so the best frequency depends on target depth.

Quick check

Which probe choice suits a deep abdominal organ?

Lower frequency ultrasound is attenuated less and reaches deeper tissue.

Ultrasound combines soft-tissue contrast with live imaging. Operators choose output and exposure to keep heating and cavitation risks low.

Quick check

Why is ordinary ultrasound poor for imaging through the skull?

Bone has a large impedance mismatch and attenuates sound, so little useful signal reaches the brain.
