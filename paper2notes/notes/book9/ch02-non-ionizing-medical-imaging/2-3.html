ATotal internal reflection

At the critical angle, the refracted ray travels along the boundary. Above that angle, light is totally internally reflected. This requires light to travel from higher refractive index to lower refractive index.

\[n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2,\qquad \sin c=\frac{n_2}{n_1}\quad(n_1>n_2)\]

Quick check

Which condition is required for total internal reflection?

Both conditions are essential: travel toward lower index, and incidence angle greater than the critical angle.

BOptical fibres

An optical fibre has a high-index core surrounded by lower-index cladding. Light entering within the acceptance angle is refracted into the core and repeatedly reflects at the boundary. The cladding prevents light from escaping into the surroundings.

\[\sin\theta_{\max}=n_{\mathrm{core}}\cos c\quad \text{(entry from air)}\]
Worked example

Critical angle

  1. Let \(n_{core}=1.52\) and \(n_{clad}=1.48\).
  2. \(\sin c=1.48/1.52\), so \(c\approx 80.7^\circ\).
  3. \(\sin\theta_{max}=1.52\cos c\), giving \(\theta_{max}\approx14.0^\circ\).

Quick check

Why is the cladding index lower than the core index?

The index step allows rays in the core to reflect internally when their incidence angle is large enough.

CEndoscopes and fibre bundles

BundleFunction
Coherentcarries the image; fibre order is preserved
Incoherentcarries illumination to the body cavity
Fine fibresmore image points and finer detail
Extra channelspass tools, air or water

An objective lens forms an image on a coherent bundle. Each fibre carries one bright or dim point to the far end, rebuilding a mosaic. Finer, more closely packed fibres can resolve smaller details.

Quick check

Which fibre bundle carries the image from inside the body?

A coherent bundle preserves the spatial order of fibres, so the image points remain in the right places.
