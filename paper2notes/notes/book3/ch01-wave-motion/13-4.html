Particle motion and wave graphs

AParticle motion and phase

  • Every particle oscillates with the same frequency and amplitude as the source; they just start at different times.
  • A particle at a crest or trough is momentarily at rest. A particle passing its rest position moves fastest.
  • While one particle makes one full oscillation, the wave moves on by exactly one wavelength.
Definition

Phase describes the stage a particle has reached in its oscillation. Particles in phase always move the same way and are a whole number of wavelengths apart. Particles in antiphase always move opposite ways and are an odd number of half wavelengths apart.

Which way is it moving?

Sketch the waveform a moment later, shifted a little in the direction of travel. Each particle moves to the new curve: up if the new curve is above it, down if below.

Quick check

A transverse wave travels to the right. Particle P is on the rest line, halfway between a crest on its left and a trough on its right. How is P moving now?

A. The crest is moving towards P, so P is rising, and at the rest line it is moving fastest. B is right for a particle with the trough on its left; C treats the particle as travelling with the wave; D is true only at a crest or trough.

  • The slope of an s–t graph is the particle's velocity: zero at a peak, steepest where \(s = 0\).
  • For a transverse wave the s–d graph has the same shape as the wave itself.
  • To get \(v\) you need both graphs (or one graph plus a given speed): \(v = \lambda / T\).
Worked example

Worked example

A particle's s–t graph peaks at 0.15 m and repeats every 2.0 s. The wave travels at 0.30 m s−1. Find the amplitude and the wavelength.

  1. Amplitude = highest displacement = 0.15 m.
  2. Period \(T = 2.0\) s, read from one full cycle on the time axis.
  3. \( \lambda = vT = 0.30 \times 2.0 = 0.60\ \text{m} \)

Quick check

(a) A single s–d graph is enough to find the frequency of a wave.

False. A snapshot has no time axis; you also need the speed or an s–t graph.

(b) Where an s–t graph reaches a peak, the particle is momentarily at rest.

True. The slope (velocity) is zero at the top of the curve.

CLongitudinal waves on an s–d graph

  • Particles at the centre of a compression or rarefaction have \(s = 0\); look at the neighbours to tell which. Left neighbour moved right and right neighbour moved left: they close in, so it is a compression.
  • Particles with the largest displacement (\(s = \pm A\)) are momentarily at rest.

Quick check

A longitudinal wave travels to the right. Particle R sits at the centre of a compression. Right is positive. Which describes R now?

D. At a compression centre the particle is at its rest position and moving fastest in the direction of travel. A describes a particle at maximum displacement; B mixes zero displacement with zero velocity; C describes a rarefaction centre.
