ATransverse waves

Definition

In a transverse wave the particles of the medium oscillate perpendicular to the direction the wave travels. Its waveform has crests (highest points) and troughs (lowest points).

  • Examples: a wave sent along a rope or a stretched spring by shaking it sideways, ripples on water, and every electromagnetic wave (light, microwaves, radio).
  • A transverse pulse keeps a steady speed as it shrinks. Shaking harder or faster does not make it travel faster.

BLongitudinal waves

Definition

In a longitudinal wave the particles oscillate along (parallel to) the direction the wave travels. Its waveform has compressions (particles crowded together) and rarefactions (particles spread out).

Earthquakes

An earthquake sends out both kinds. The longitudinal P-wave is faster and arrives first; the transverse S-wave arrives later. The gap between the two arrivals grows with distance from the source.

Quick check

Sam stands due east of a loudspeaker. As its sound passes him, the air particles near him oscillate

B. Sound in air is longitudinal, so the particles move along the line from the speaker to Sam. A and C are transverse directions; D describes neither kind.

Quick check

(a) A longitudinal wave has crests and troughs.

False. Its pattern is compressions and rarefactions; crests and troughs belong to transverse waves.

(b) Light from a street lamp is a transverse wave.

True. Every electromagnetic wave is transverse.

(c) Pushing and pulling one end of a slinky along its length makes a transverse wave.

False. The coils move along the slinky, the same line the wave travels, so the wave is longitudinal.
