Chapter 13

Summary: Wave Motion

Formulas, definitions and the traps that cost marks.

Goals

Students should be able to:

  • interpret wave motion in terms of oscillation

  • realise waves as transmitting energy without transferring matter

  • distinguish between transverse and longitudinal waves

  • describe wave motion in terms of waveform, crest, trough, compression, rarefaction, wavefront, phase, displacement, amplitude, period, frequency, wavelength and wave speed

  • present information on displacement-time and displacement-distance graphs for travelling waves

  • determine factors affecting the speed of propagation of waves along stretched strings or springs

  • apply \( f = \frac{1}{T} \) and \( v = f\lambda \) to solve problems

4Traps that cost marks

Energy, not matter

A wave does not carry the water, air or rope along. Particles oscillate about fixed rest positions.

Speed belongs to the medium

Shaking faster raises \(f\) and shortens \(\lambda\); shaking harder raises \(A\). Neither changes \(v\) on the same string.

Crest to trough is 2A

Amplitude is measured from the rest line. Compression to next rarefaction is \(\lambda/2\).

Particles do not travel

On a transverse wave the particles move only up and down. Never draw an arrow along the wave for a particle.

Compression is not maximum displacement

At a compression centre \(s = 0\) and the particle moves fastest, forwards. The particles with \(s = \pm A\) are at rest.

Right graph for the job

Wavelength comes from an s–d graph, period from an s–t graph. One graph alone cannot give the speed.
