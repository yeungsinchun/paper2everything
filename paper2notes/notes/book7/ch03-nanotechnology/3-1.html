Section 3.1

Matter waves

A moving particle has a wavelength set by its momentum. Matter waves become visible when their wavelength matches a structure.

In short

λ = h/p = h/(mv) for non-relativistic motion.

Diffraction is visible when wavelength and structure spacing are comparable.

A standing wave around an orbit requires an integer number of wavelengths.

AThe de Broglie relation BMatter-wave diffraction CStable orbital waves

AThe de Broglie relation

Matter has wave-like properties. Its wavelength decreases as momentum increases.

\[\lambda=\frac{h}{p}=\frac{h}{mv}\]

h is Planck’s constant, m is mass and v is speed. The second form assumes p = mv.

Worked example

Apply the idea

A 150 eV electron has λ = h/√(2mK) ≈ 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.100 nm. Convert eV to joules before using SI constants.

Quick check

What change shortens a particle’s de Broglie wavelength?

C. Higher momentum means smaller λ. Increasing mass at fixed speed also increases momentum.

BMatter-wave diffraction

A wave diffracts when passing through an opening or crystal lattice. The effect becomes apparent when wavelength and spacing are similar.

Compare scales

Strong diffraction needs wavelength near the opening or repeating spacing. A much shorter wavelength produces negligible spreading at that scale.

Worked example

Apply the idea

Electrons diffract in a crystal because their wavelength can approach the lattice spacing. A ball’s wavelength is far smaller than a doorway, so its diffraction is undetectable.

Quick check

Which condition makes electron diffraction observable?

A. The electron wavelength must be comparable with the crystal spacing.

CStable orbital waves

A closed wave reinforces itself only if it joins in phase after each circuit. This gives a model for discrete allowed orbits.

\[2\pi r=n\lambda,\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots\]

Using λ = h/(mv) gives mvr = nh/(2π), Bohr’s angular-momentum condition.

Worked example

Apply the idea

For r = 0.20 nm and n = 2, λ = 2πr/n ≈ 0.63 nm.

Quick check

What condition allows the wave around an orbit to reinforce itself?

A. The circumference must contain an integer number of wavelengths.
