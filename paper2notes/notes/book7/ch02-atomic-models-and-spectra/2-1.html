Quick check

Why is the α-scattering experiment done in a vacuum, with a very thin foil?

  1. α particles have a short range in air.
  2. A thin foil means each α particle is scattered by at most one nucleus.
  3. A vacuum stops the gold foil from oxidising.

D. Air would stop the α particles within centimetres, and a thick foil would mix many small deflections together. Gold does not oxidise, so (3) is not a reason.

BWhat the α particles did

ObservationConclusion
Most α particles pass straight throughMost of the atom is empty space.
Some are deflected through large anglesThe positive charge is concentrated in a very small region.
A very few (about 1 in 8000) bounce backThat region, the nucleus, also holds almost all the mass: a light electron could not turn an α particle round.
Rutherford's model (1911)

An atom has a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus holding almost all its mass. Electrons orbit the nucleus, held by electric attraction, as planets orbit the Sun. Most of the atom is empty space: the nucleus is about \(10^{-15}\) m across, the atom about \(10^{-10}\) m.

Quick check

An α particle is fired at a gold nucleus. Which statement about its path is correct?

B. The repulsion grows as the α particle gets closer, so a closer approach means a sharper turn. A gets the force direction wrong: both are positive. C is wrong because the repulsion acts at a distance; the α particle never touches the nucleus. D contradicts the observations.

Quick check

(a) The experiment showed that the nucleus contains protons and neutrons.

False. It showed only a small, dense, positive centre. Protons and neutrons were found in later experiments.

(b) Most of the volume of an atom is empty.

True. That is why most α particles pass straight through the foil.

Quick check

Why should an electron orbiting a nucleus lose energy, according to classical physics?

C. Circular motion has a centripetal acceleration even at constant speed, and accelerating charges radiate. A and B invent contact and friction. D has the force the wrong way: the nucleus attracts the electron.

DScattering experiments today

Firing fast particles at a target and watching how they scatter is still the main way to see inside matter.

ExperimentDiscovery
α particles on gold foil (1909–1911)The nucleus
α particles on nitrogen (1919)The proton, knocked out of nuclei
α particles on beryllium (1932)The neutron, a neutral particle in the nucleus
High-energy electrons on protons (1960s)Quarks: protons and neutrons have smaller parts
Why bigger machines

To see smaller details, a probe needs more energy. Particle accelerators are giant scattering experiments.

Quick check

Which particles were discovered in scattering experiments?

  1. Protons
  2. Neutrons
  3. Quarks

D. All three were found by firing particles at targets: protons and neutrons with α particles, quarks with high-energy electrons.
