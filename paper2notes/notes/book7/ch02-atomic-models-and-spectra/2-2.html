AContinuous, emission and absorption spectra

SpectrumSourceLooks like

Absorption lineWhite light passed through a cooler low-pressure gasContinuous band crossed by dark lines
Definition

A line spectrum contains only certain separate wavelengths, the spectral lines. Bright lines form an emission spectrum; dark lines on a continuous band form an absorption spectrum.

Quick check

Which source gives a line spectrum?

C. A sodium lamp holds a thin vapour whose atoms emit on their own, so it shows sodium's bright yellow lines. A, B and D are hot dense matter, which glows with a continuous spectrum.

BEvery element has its own lines

Definition

Spectral analysis identifies the elements in a glowing gas by matching its lines to the known line patterns of elements.

An element is present in a mixture only if all of its lines appear in the mixture's spectrum. One missing line rules it out.

Quick check

The combined spectrum contains every line from two reference samples. Which pair best fits the plotted lines?

B. The observed lines agree with the X and Z references. Including Y would add spectral features that the plot does not show.

CAbsorption lines and the Sun

  • A gas absorbs only the wavelengths it can emit: its absorption lines match its emission lines exactly (Fig. 2.4).
  • Sunlight shows hundreds of dark Fraunhofer lines. Cooler gas in the Sun's outer layers, mostly hydrogen, absorbs those wavelengths from the light of the hot surface below.
  • Matching Fraunhofer lines to laboratory spectra showed that the Sun is mostly hydrogen and helium.

Quick check

(a) A gas whose emission spectrum has a line at 589 nm will absorb light of wavelength 589 nm.

True. Emission and absorption use the same energy gaps, so the lines sit at the same wavelengths.

(b) Dark lines appear because the absorbed light is destroyed and never re-emitted.

False. The atoms do re-emit, but in all directions, so little of that light travels on in the original beam.

DLine spectra mean energy levels

Definition

An atom can have only certain separate energies, its energy levels. It emits or absorbs a photon only when it jumps between two levels, and the photon carries exactly the energy difference.

\[ hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = E_{\text{upper}} - E_{\text{lower}} \]

A larger gap gives a higher-frequency, shorter-wavelength photon. A photon whose energy matches no gap passes through the gas untouched.

Quick check

What do line spectra tell us directly about atoms?

  1. An atom's energy takes only certain separate values.
  2. An atom emits and absorbs light as photons.
  3. An atom has electrons orbiting a nucleus.

A. Sharp lines mean fixed photon energies, which need both photons and fixed atomic energies. A spectrum says nothing about where the charges sit; the orbiting picture came from α scattering, so (3) is out.

Worked example: a hydrogen line and a grating
Worked example

Hydrogen emits a red line of wavelength 656 nm. (a) How much energy does an atom lose when it emits this photon? (b) The light falls on a grating with 600 lines per mm. At what angle is the first-order red line seen?

  1. \(E = hc/\lambda = (6.63 \times 10^{-34})(3.00 \times 10^{8}) / (656 \times 10^{-9}) = 3.03 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}\).
  2. In eV: \(3.03 \times 10^{-19} / 1.60 \times 10^{-19} = 1.89\ \mathrm{eV}\).
  3. Grating spacing \(d = 10^{-3} / 600 = 1.67 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}\).
  4. \(d \sin\theta = m\lambda\): \(\sin\theta = (1)(656 \times 10^{-9}) / 1.67 \times 10^{-6} = 0.394\).

(a) 1.89 eV (\(3.03 \times 10^{-19}\) J); (b) \(\theta = 23.2^\circ\)
