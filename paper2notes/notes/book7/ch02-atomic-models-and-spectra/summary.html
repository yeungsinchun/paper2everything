2Definitions

Nucleus
The tiny, dense, positive centre of an atom that holds almost all its mass.
Continuous spectrum
An unbroken band of all wavelengths, from hot solids, liquids and dense gases.
Emission spectrum
Bright lines at certain wavelengths, from a hot low-pressure gas.
Absorption spectrum
Dark lines on a continuous band, left when white light passes through a cool gas.
Spectral analysis
Identifying elements by matching their spectral lines.
Energy level
One of the separate energies an atom is allowed to have.
Stationary state
A state with the electron in an allowed orbit, where it does not radiate.
Quantized
Restricted to certain separate values.
Ground state
The lowest energy level, \(n = 1\).
Excited state
Any level above the ground state.
Excitation energy
The energy to raise an atom from one level to a higher one.
Ionization energy
The least energy to remove the electron from an atom in its ground state.
Spectral series
All the lines from jumps that end on the same level.

4Traps that cost marks

Repelled, not attracted

An α particle and a nucleus are both positive. The α particle curves away and never touches the nucleus.

Count from n = 1

The first excited state is \(n = 2\), the second is \(n = 3\). Read the wording before you substitute.

Ionize to infinity

Ionizing from level \(n\) needs \(0 - E_n\), not \(E_n - E_1\).

Photon: exact; electron: enough

A photon must match a gap exactly. An electron needs at least the gap and keeps the rest.

Bigger jump, shorter wavelength

\(\lambda = hc/\Delta E\). The series limit is the shortest wavelength in a series.

Dark lines still get re-emitted

Absorbed light is re-emitted in all directions, so little reaches the observer in the beam.

5Last checks

Quick check

What is the ratio of the least energy needed to ionize a hydrogen atom in the \(n = 2\) state to that in the \(n = 4\) state?

D. Ionization energy from level \(n\) is \(13.6/n^2\): \(3.40 : 0.85 = 4 : 1\). B forgets the square; A and C invert the ratio.

Quick check

A hydrogen atom has a total energy of \(-0.378\) eV. What is its quantum number?

B. \(n^2 = 13.6 / 0.378 = 36\), so \(n = 6\). D stops at \(n^2\).
