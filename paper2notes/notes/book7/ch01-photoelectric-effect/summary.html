2Definitions

Photoelectric effect
Electrons are emitted from a metal when light of high enough frequency falls on it.
Photoelectron
An electron emitted in the photoelectric effect.
Photocurrent
Current carried by photoelectrons reaching the anode; proportional to their number per second.
Saturation current
The steady photocurrent when every photoelectron is collected.
Stopping potential
The smallest reverse voltage that stops all photoelectrons reaching the anode.
Threshold frequency
The lowest frequency that emits photoelectrons from a given metal.
Photon
A packet of electromagnetic energy \(hf\).
Work function
The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a metal surface.
Electronvolt
The energy one electron gains crossing 1 V: \(1.60 \times 10^{-19}\) J.

5Traps that cost marks

Brighter is not faster

Raising intensity gives more photoelectrons per second, never a larger \(K_{\max}\) or \(V_s\).

Stopping is not blocking emission

At the stopping potential the cathode still emits. The electrons are turned back before the anode.

Waiting does not help

Below \(f_0\) no electron escapes, however long or brightly the light shines. One photon must do the job alone.

Minimum, not every

The work function is the least energy to escape. Most photoelectrons leave with less than \(K_{\max}\).

eV or J, not both

In \(hf - \varphi\), convert both terms to the same unit before subtracting.

Longer wavelength, smaller photon

\(E = hc/\lambda\): a threshold wavelength is a maximum, a threshold frequency a minimum.

6Last checks

Quick check

A photon gives a metal electron 3.1 eV. The fastest emitted electron has 0.8 eV of kinetic energy. Find the work function.

C. Energy conservation gives \(\varphi = 3.1 - 0.8 = 2.3\) eV. Subtract the emitted electron's kinetic energy from the photon's energy.

Quick check

Which change would let photoelectrons be emitted from a metal that emits none under red light?

B. Only a larger photon, that is a higher frequency, can exceed the work function. A and C are wave-theory ideas. D helps collect electrons but cannot free any.
