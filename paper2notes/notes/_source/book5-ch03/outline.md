## 27.1 Nuclear fission and chain reaction (printed pp.90–94, Figs 27.1–27.4, Ex 27.1)

### A. Fission is not decay

- **Visual:** Fig.27.1 3D: U-235 sphere + slow neutron in → two fragments + 3 neutrons + KE arrows. Toggle fast vs slow neutron (fast bounces off).
- **Text (minimal):** Heavy nucleus splits into lighter nuclei **when hit by slow neutron**. Induced, not spontaneous. Products vary (Xe/Sr etc); always lighter + neutrons + KE (fragments repel).
- **Check:** QB PHY15031101 (which is fission?), PHY15031109 (fragmented into lighter ones, random false). Checkpoint: Example 27.1 table.

### B. Why fission is unlike α decay

- **Visual:** Side-by-side: left fission (neutron in, branching neutrons out); right α decay (nucleus alone emits He, random, half-life fixed). Animate half-life clock vs controlled rate.
- **Text:** Decay spontaneous, random, rate = fixed half-life, cannot be controlled; fission triggered, rate controlled by neutron supply, products different each time.
- **Check:** Example 27.1 solution paraphrased as T/F; QB PHY15031104/102 chain-condition trap.

### C. Chain reaction and critical mass — branching

- **Visual:** Fig.27.2 branching tree: one fission → 3 neutrons → each may hit another U-235 or escape. Slider for concentration / critical mass (subcritical vs supercritical). Domino pyramid Try This analogue.
- **Text:** Need average ≥1 neutron causes next fission. If U-235 too dilute or mass too small, neutrons escape → dies out. Critical mass depends on density/purity. Natural 0.7% → enriched >90% bomb, ~3% reactor → reactor cannot explode like bomb.
- **Check:** QB PHY15031115 (cut sphere loses chain — neutrons escape), PHY15031102 branch.

### D. Reactor: making the branch stay at 1

- **Visual:** Pressurized water reactor cutaway (fuel rods, control rods absorb neutrons, moderator/water slows neutrons, boiler→turbine). Animate inserting control rods → branching drops <1 → power down.
- **Text:** Three jobs: fuel provides fission; control rods absorb neutrons to hold k≈1; moderator slows fast neutrons to slow capture. Water circuit carries heat. Fuel low enrichment, so even uncontrolled cannot reach bomb supercritical.
- **Check:** QB PHY15031108 (electricity generation = controlled fission), Exercise Q9 Daya Bay fuel consumption (link to mass-energy later).

## 27.2 Nuclear fusion — the Sun's way (printed pp.95–98, Figs 27.5–27.11)

### A. Fusion is the opposite

- **Visual:** Fig.27.5 D+T → He+n + KE arrows. Fig.27.6 two positively charged light nuclei repelling (Coulomb). Show slow nuclei bounce, fast nuclei overcome + fuse.
- **Text:** Light nuclei fuse to heavier. Opposite of fission but same outcome: lighter products heavier-bound → mass defect → energy. Example D+T releases ~17.6 MeV (vs fission ~200 MeV per reaction, but per kg fusion gives ~5× more — summary mistake).
- **Check:** QB PHY15031103/15031123 (which is fusion? spontaneous false, needs high T not spontaneous).

### B. Why fusion needs a star

- **Visual:** Temperature slider: at 300 K nuclei far apart; at 10⁷ K they collide with enough KE to overcome electrostatic repulsion → fuse, glow. Sun core ~1.5×10⁷ K, millions tonnes H/s.
- **Text:** Repulsion → needs huge KE → huge temperature. Sun achieves this by gravity. Solar energy we feel is EM from these fusions.
- **Check:** Checkpoint 2(d) "fusion needs very high temperature" T/F; QB PHY15031109 C false.

### C. Hydrogen bomb vs fusion reactor

- **Visual:** Fig.27.9 bomb inside bomb: fission primary → heat → fusion secondary (uncontrolled, 1000× fission bomb, Tsar Bomba fireball). Vs Fig.27.10 tokamak magnetic confinement (challenges).
- **Text:** Bomb uses fission trigger for T; uncontrolled. Reactor wants controlled but no container holds 10⁸ K plasma; magnetic fields tried; no commercial plant yet. Waste: fusion He non-radioactive vs fission spent fuel long half-lives (I-129 15.7 Myr) pools → underground.
- **Check:** QB PHY15032108 Reaction I vs II Sun/reactor; Chapter Exercise for/against paragraphs.

### D. Fission vs fusion at a glance

- **Visual:** One table-or graphic: fission heavy→lighter+neutrons, neutron trigger; fusion light→heavier+neutron, high T; both release energy, both Δm<0.
- **Text:** Keep per-reaction vs per-kg distinction to avoid summary mistake.
- **Check:** HPC: 1 kg H vs 1 kg U energy ratio ~5.

## 27.3 Mass-energy: where the energy was (printed pp.99–106, Figs 27.14, Table 27.1, Ex 27.2–27.5)

### A. Missing mass becomes energy

- **Visual:** 3.js mass-energy animation: balance scale — left pan "before" masses, right pan "after" masses lighter; needle tips → flash → energy bar rises with ΔE=Δmc². Toggle fission vs fusion example. Kinetic energy of fragments / He+n shown as motion.
- **Text (minimal):** Before heavier than after by Δm (mass defect). E=Δmc², c=3×10⁸ m/s. 1 g missing →9×10¹³ J! Energy appears as KE of products then heat → electricity. Applies to fission, fusion, even α decay.
- **Check:** Checkpoint 1(a) fusion products heavier false; 1(b) energy from collisions false; Example 27.2 U vs coal 2.57×10⁶ kg coal =1 kg U.

### B. Units that fit the atom

- **Visual:** Scale strip: 1 u =1.661×10⁻²⁷ kg = 1/12 C-12; 1 eV =1.60×10⁻¹⁹ J; 1 u c² =931 MeV conversion drawn as double arrow. Table 27.1 masses.
- **Text:** Joule/kg too big; use eV for energy, u for mass. Because E=mc², u can be quoted as energy: 1 u ↔931 MeV.
- **Check:** QB PHY15031105 (1u =1/12 C-12 vs neutron mass), PHY15031117 man 53 kg →3.19×10²⁸ u, PHY15031106 1000 MeV →1.07 u, PHY15031116 which units can be energy (u and eV, not Bq).

### C. Working with Δm

- **Visual:** Worked examples step animation: list reactant masses → sum → product masses → subtract → Δm in u → ×931 → MeV → ×1.6×10⁻¹³ → J → multiply by number of nuclei (via Avogadro or mass/N_A) → total J / power.
- **Text / equations on screen:**
  - Δm = Σm(reactants) − Σm(products) in u
  - ΔE = Δm ×931 MeV = Δm ×1.661×10⁻²⁷×c² J
  - For N reactions: E_total = N·ΔE; N = m_fuel/(m_one nucleus in kg) or (m/M)·N_A
  - Example 27.3 D+T 0.0189 u →17.6 MeV, 5 g →1.69×10¹¹ J
  - Example 27.4 La/Br 166.37 MeV →0.1787 u → neutron 1.0087 u, 5 g/s →3.4×10⁷ W
  - Example 27.5 Pu-238 α 0.0060 u →5.586 MeV →8.94×10⁻¹³ J, activity for 300 W at 10% →3.36×10¹⁴ Bq
- **Check:** QB PHY15031104 (2× mass →2× energy, not 9×), PHY15031120 proton mass to MeV/J, Checkpoint 2 calculation.

### D. Binding energy in one picture (enrichment)

- **Visual:** Average binding energy per nucleon vs A curve (Fig p105 enrichment) with peak ~Fe-56 ~8.8 MeV, fission left high A drops, fusion left low A rises → both go toward peak. No detailed numbers needed for core.
- **Text:** Both go to more tightly bound nuclei; need energy to separate nucleons = binding energy. For core just note trend; details in Energy & Use.
- **Check:** Checkpoint 4 D vs P story — enrichment glimpse only.
