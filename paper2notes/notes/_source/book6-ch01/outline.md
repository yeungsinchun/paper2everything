## 1.1 Let's begin (printed pp.4-10, Figs 1.1-1.13, Example 1.1, Checkpoint 1)

### A. A journey in steps of 100

- **Visual:** static SVG "scale ladder": a log ruler from 10^1 m to 10^25 m with each journey step marked (street 13 m, city 1.3 km, Hong Kong 130 km, Earth 1.3x10^7 m, Earth-Moon 10^9 m, Sun-Earth 10^11 m, solar system 10^13 m, nearest stars 10^17 m, star cluster 10^19 m, Milky Way 10^21 m, galaxy clusters 10^23 m, filaments 10^25 m). Each tick is a factor of 100 wider than the last.
- **Text:** every step widens the view 100 times; 12 steps cover 24 powers of ten. Light from the nearest star (Proxima Centauri) takes 4.2 years. The Milky Way is about 10^21 m (~10^5 ly) across and holds more than 10^11 stars.
- **Check:** order objects by size (Earth < solar system < Milky Way < Local Group); spot the step that first shows other stars.
- Leave the History box (Apollo 11, Voyager 1, unmanned probes table) as one short "distance vs travel time" line, not a table copy.

### B. Units for huge distances: AU, light year, parsec

- **Visual:** animated stage `light-time`: a pulse of light leaves the Sun and the clock counts light-minutes as it passes Mercury, Venus, Earth (8.3 min), Mars (12.7 min), Jupiter (43 min). Teaches "light-travel time is a distance". Finite clip, replay.
- **Definitions:** 1 AU = mean Earth-Sun distance = 1.496x10^11 m. 1 ly = distance light travels in vacuum in one year = 9.461x10^15 m = 6.32x10^4 AU. 1 pc = 3.26 ly (defined in Ch.4 via parallax).
- **Formula:** 1 ly = c x (1 year in s) = (2.998x10^8)(365.24 x 24 x 3600).
- **Check:** "light year is a unit of time" trap; convert 30 AU to m; order 1000 km, 0.1 AU, 10 light-minutes.
- **Worked example (collapsed):** a star 25 ly away in AU and m; light time Sun to Jupiter (5.2 AU), our own numbers not the textbook's (use 9.5 AU Saturn and 8.6 ly Sirius).

## 1.2 Celestial body (printed pp.11-19, Figs 1.14-1.21, Checkpoint 2, Exercise)

### A. Planets, satellites and small bodies

- **Visual:** static SVG of the eight planets in order from the Sun, sizes not to scale but ranked, terrestrial group (rock, small, thin atmosphere, few moons) vs Jovian group (H/He, large, thick atmosphere, many moons); dwarf planets, asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, comet with tail pointing away from the Sun.
- **Definitions:** planet = orbits a star, not luminous, seen by reflected light. Satellite = orbits a planet (Moon is Earth's only natural one; Mercury and Venus have none).
- **Table:** terrestrial vs Jovian planets.
- **Check:** which shine by their own light; which body orbits which.

### B. Stars, star clusters and nebulae

- **Visual:** animated stage `nebula`: a cold, dense cloud of gas and dust contracts under its own gravity, breaks into clumps and the clumps light up as stars of one cluster. Finite clip, replay.
- **Definitions:** star = luminous, makes its energy by nuclear fusion. Star cluster = stars bound by gravity, born from the same nebula. Nebula = cloud of gas and dust between stars; a birthplace of stars (and debris of dying stars).
- **Check:** which object is the birthplace of stars; what holds a cluster together (gravity, not electric or nuclear force).

### C. Galaxies and galaxy clusters

- **Visual:** static SVG trio: spiral (disc with arms, the Milky Way type), elliptical (oval, little gas and dust), irregular (no definite shape, e.g. Magellanic Clouds). Local Group note: Milky Way and Andromeda (M31, 2.5 million ly, the farthest object seen by the naked eye) are its two largest members.
- **Definitions:** galaxy = huge system of stars, gas and dust held by gravity; galaxy cluster = group of tens to thousands of galaxies; ours is the Local Group.
- **Check:** is everything we see at night inside the Milky Way? (No: Andromeda is outside.)

### D. Superclusters, filaments and voids

- **Visual:** static SVG nested-scale diagram: satellite < planet < star < nebula < star cluster < galaxy < galaxy cluster < supercluster < filaments, as nested boxes with size labels. Plus a schematic web of filaments with empty voids.
- **Text:** superclusters (clusters of clusters) join into filaments over 10^8 ly; voids hold few galaxies; filaments and voids are the largest known structures.
- **Check:** arrange nebula, planet, galaxy, star in ascending size; is a star cluster in the solar system?
