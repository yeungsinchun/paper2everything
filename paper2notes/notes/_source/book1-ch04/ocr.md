The gas laws we discussed in the last section tell us how the

2. The volume of a gas is the space in which the gas molecules

Ideal gas and real gas

Ideal gas

In the discussion above, we have made several assumptions
(p. 166). A gas that satisfies the above assumptions is known as
an ideal gas. It turns out that such a gas obeys the ideal gas law.

The main assumption, which in effect defines an ideal gas in
microscopic level, is that the range of the intermolecular force is
very small compared with the distance between gas molecules.
In short,

there is no interaction between ideal gas molecules.

Since the gas molecules in an ideal gas do not interact with
each other (except during a collision), they have no molecular
PE. The internal energy of an ideal gas is simply equal to its
molecular KE:

low pressure. This is because at high T and low p, the molecules
are far apart, and therefore they do not interact strongly with
one another.
