## 4.1 Gas pressure
- Contrast force and pressure when contact area changes.
- Explain pressure from wall collisions: more frequent or harder impacts raise pressure.
- Explain atmospheric pressure and pressure imbalance using arrows on a surface or piston.
- Calculate pressure and balanced piston forces with consistent SI units.

## 4.2 Gas laws
- Choose the relation only after identifying which variable and gas amount stay fixed.
- Convert Celsius values to Kelvin for every gas-law ratio.
- Apply Boyle, pressure and Charles laws to paired states; distinguish inverse and direct proportionality.
- Use \(pV=nRT\) for suitable ideal-gas problems and keep units consistent.

## 4.3 Kinetic theory
- Link temperature to average molecular kinetic energy and pressure to wall collisions.
- Use collision frequency, speed and number density to explain macroscopic changes.
- Identify the assumptions and limits of the jumping-beads analogy and ideal-gas model.
- Extension checks may use \(pV=\frac13Nm\langle v^2\rangle\) or rms speed only if the published syllabus requires them.
