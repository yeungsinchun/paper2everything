## 1.1 Temperature and thermometers (printed pp.4-12)

### A. Temperature and thermometric properties
- **Visual:** temperature ladder from absolute zero to the Sun's surface (static SVG, log-ish spacing, labelled points only; no photos). A glowing bar that changes colour with temperature as the first "property that changes with hotness".
- **Text:** temperature = degree of hotness; a thermometric property is any property that changes steadily with temperature (liquid length, resistance, gas pressure/volume, colour of glow, thermocouple current).
- **Check:** which graph of R against θ could be used for a thermometer (monotonic, not flat).

### B. The Celsius scale: two fixed points
- **Visual:** liquid-in-glass thermometer stage: bulb in melting ice → column at the lower mark; bulb in steam → upper mark; interval split into 100 divisions (animated, finite clip with Replay).
- **Text:** ice point 0 °C = pure melting ice at 1 atm; steam point 100 °C = pure boiling water at 1 atm; both stable and reproducible. Narrow capillary + big bulb = sensitive.
- **Check:** choose the fixed points; why a narrow capillary.

### C. Calibration and the linear (proportion) method
- **Visual:** interactive calibration graph: drag a slider for θ, the reading on a linear property moves; dashed similar triangles.
- **Formula:** θ = (X_θ − X_0)/(X_100 − X_0) × 100 °C for a property X that varies linearly (state the assumption).
- **Worked:** Example 1.1 style (new numbers): length 4 cm at 5 °C, 22 cm at 95 °C.
- **Check:** wrongly marked thermometer (reads 9 and 109 at fixed points) style; resistance thermometer linear.
- **Tip:** offset the zero-point value; calibration graph needed when the property is not linear.

## 1.2 Heat and internal energy (printed pp.13-19)

### A. Temperature as average molecular KE
- **Visual:** two boxes of jiggling molecules (2D canvas): cold box slow, hot box fast; speeds vary within each box.
- **Text:** temperature measures the average KE per molecule of random motion; not total KE.
- **Check:** two cups same temperature, different mass → same average KE, different total KE.

### B. Internal energy = KE + PE
- **Visual:** molecule pair "spring bond" diagram; bar showing total = KE part + PE part; heating raises KE (temperature) or PE (change of state).
- **Text:** internal energy = sum of random KE and intermolecular PE of all molecules; depends on mass, temperature, state.
- **Check:** T/F set on what internal energy depends on.

### C. Heat flows hot → cold until thermal equilibrium
- **Visual:** hot tea + cold cup stage: energy packets flow, two temperature bars converge, flow stops at equal temperature (finite clip with Replay).
- **Text:** heat = energy transferred because of a temperature difference; a body contains internal energy, not heat; rate grows with temperature difference.
- **Check:** direction of flow decided by temperature, not internal energy (2015/03 and 2021/02 test this). Ice at 0 °C + water at 0 °C: no flow.

## 1.3 Ways of heat transfer (printed pp.20-36)

### A. Convection
- **Visual:** water tank heated at one corner: convection loop of dyed particles (2D canvas loop). Sea breeze vs land breeze toggle.
- **Text:** fluid heated → expands → less dense → rises; cooler denser fluid sinks to replace it → current. Needs a fluid and gravity.
- **Check:** where to put a heater/freezer coil; smoke detector on the ceiling.

### B. Conduction
- **Visual:** rod heated at one end: atoms vibrate harder, vibration passes along; metal version adds free electrons that carry energy fast (pane pair, loop).
- **Text:** conduction = energy passed by collisions between neighbouring particles; free electrons make metals good conductors; trapped air makes good insulators.
- **Table:** relative conductivity ladder (silver/copper ... air).
- **Check:** metal feels colder than wood at the same temperature (rate of heat flow from hand).

### C. Radiation
- **Visual:** emitter/absorber stage: dull black vs shiny can, IR wavy arrows; slider toggles "can hotter / colder than room" to switch net emitter vs net absorber.
- **Text:** all objects emit infrared; hotter → more and higher-frequency radiation; needs no medium; dull black = good absorber and emitter, shiny = good reflector, poor absorber and emitter.
- **Table:** dull black vs shiny.
- **Check:** survival blanket, black fins (2023/01 idea), flasks black vs silvery (2026/01 idea).

## 1.4 Devices controlling heat flow (printed pp.37-39)

### A. Vacuum flask
- **Visual:** labelled cross-section SVG of a vacuum flask with the three blocked paths.
- **Table:** feature → transfer reduced → why.
- **Check:** keeps cold things cold too (2014/01, 2016/01 ideas).

### B. Speeding heat flow up: heat sink, radiator fins
- **Visual:** CPU heat sink SVG: conduction into fins, convection by fan.
- **Text:** good contact (conduction), large area (fins), forced convection.

### C. Greenhouse and greenhouse effect
- **Visual:** greenhouse stage: short-wave sunlight in, long-wave IR partly trapped, warm air trapped.
- **Text:** glass passes visible light, blocks much IR; closed glass also blocks convection (often the bigger effect); atmosphere CO₂ and water vapour play the same role.
- **Check:** car parked in sunshine (2015/01 idea).
