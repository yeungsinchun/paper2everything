## 2.1 Rate of energy transfer (printed pp.52-57)

### A. Power
- **Visual:** interactive E–t graph: power slider (0.5-3 kW); the line E = Pt and its slope triangle; second faint reference line at 1 kW.
- **Text:** P = E/t; W = J s⁻¹; kW, MW; time must be in seconds; slope of E–t = power; at constant P, E ∝ t.
- **Worked:** two kettles (new numbers), solar panel with efficiency (new numbers).
- **Check:** unit of power; energy from a varying-power P–t graph (area).

## 2.2 Heat capacity (printed pp.58-71)

### A. Heat capacity and specific heat capacity
- **Visual:** interactive heating curve: mass slider and material buttons (water, aluminium, copper) with a fixed 1 kW heater; slope = P/(mc).
- **Text:** C = E/ΔT (J °C⁻¹), body property; c = E/(mΔT) (J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹), material property; E = mcΔT; table of c values.
- **Check:** same energy, which body warms most; C depends on mass, c does not.

### B. Measuring c (electrical method)
- **Visual:** SVG of immersion heater + joulemeter + polystyrene cup / aluminium block.
- **Text:** c = (E₂ − E₁)/(m(T₂ − T₁)); precautions (immerse heater fully, lid, stir, insulate, oil in holes, use the highest temperature). Heat loss makes the measured c too large.
- **Worked:** joulemeter readings example with new numbers.

### C. Heating and cooling curves
- **Visual:** SVG: straight heating line without loss, bending line with loss; cooling curve flattening towards room temperature.
- **Text:** rate of temperature rise = P/(mc); rate of loss grows with temperature difference.
- **Check:** three heating curves, which is the heaviest block (same heater).

## 2.3 Temperature of a mixture (printed pp.72-77)

### A. Energy released = energy absorbed (+ loss)
- **Visual:** interactive mixing figure: hot water poured onto a cold body; mass slider, cold-body material buttons; temperature axis shows T_hot falling and T_cold rising to the common T; two energy bars stay equal.
- **Text:** conservation of energy; set up m₁c₁(T₁ − T) = m₂c₂(T − T₂) (+ loss); method of mixtures to find c of a solid.
- **Worked:** tea into a teapot; metal cube into water (new numbers).
- **Check:** equal masses of water at 20 °C and 40 °C; what heat loss does to a method-of-mixtures result.

## 2.4 High specific heat capacity of water (printed pp.78-79)

### A. Small temperature change for a lot of energy
- **Visual:** interactive bars: same energy into 1 kg each of water, aluminium, copper, lead; temperature rises differ.
- **Text:** coolant in engines and radiators, body temperature, coastal climate (Hong Kong vs inland), soup keeps food hot.
- **Check:** coastal vs inland daily range; wet vs dry sand.
