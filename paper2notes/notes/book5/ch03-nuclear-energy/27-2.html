Section 27.2

Fusion — what lights the Sun

The opposite of fission: two light nuclei join. The electrical repulsion you know from \( F = kq_{1}q_{2}/r^{2} \) is the whole barrier. Give the nuclei enough kinetic energy — a star does — and they touch and release even more.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • realise the release of energy in nuclear fusion

  • realise nuclear fusion as the source of solar energy

ATwo light nuclei become one heavier nucleus

Definition

Nuclear fusion is the joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus. The total mass after is smaller; the missing mass becomes kinetic energy of the products (27.3).

\[ {}^{2}_{1}\mathrm{H} + {}^{3}_{1}\mathrm{H} \rightarrow {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He} + {}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n} + \text{energy} \quad (17.6\ \mathrm{MeV}) \]
  • Per reaction fusion releases < fission (17.6 MeV < 200 MeV), but per kilogram of fuel fusion releases ~5× more — because light nuclei are so much lighter (summary bar chart fixes this common flip).
  • Both fission and fusion go toward more tightly bound nuclei (near iron-56). See 27.3 D binding curve.

Concept check

Which is a fusion reaction?

B is light+light→heavier. A is fission, C α decay, D β decay.

BWhy you need a star — the Coulomb hill

Temperature slider (KE) \( 1.5\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{K} \)
  • You already know like charges repel. That repulsion does work against the approach, converting KE → electrical potential energy. Slow nuclei run out of KE and bounce.
  • At \( \sim 10^{7}\ \mathrm{K} \) typical particle KE \( \sim kT \sim 1\ \mathrm{keV} \); with Maxwell tail many have enough to overcome tens of keV barrier. On Earth we heat deuterium/tritium deliberately; the Sun compresses hydrogen by gravity to ~\( 1.5\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{K} \) in its core and fuses ~\( 6\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{kg} \) H per second.
  • No bombardment with neutrons needed — the trigger is kinetic energy, not a particle captured. (A common confusion with fission.)

Concept check

(a) Fusion requires a very high temperature so that nuclei have enough kinetic energy to overcome electrostatic repulsion.

True — that is Fig.27.6. Unlike fission, no neutron needed; unlike decay, it is not spontaneous at our temperatures.

(b) A fusion reaction can be triggered by bombarding light nuclei with slow neutrons, like fission.

False — different trigger. Light nuclei are not neutron-capturing; they need high relative KE.

CThe Sun — sustained fusion by gravity

  • Main chain: \( 4\,{}^{1}\mathrm{H} \rightarrow {}^{4}\mathrm{He} + 2e^{+} + 2\nu_{e} \) overall; D–T used on Earth is a convenient subset.
  • Huge temperature alone is not enough — also huge pressure/gravity holds fuel together while it burns. That is why a reactor must confine hot plasma magnetically; the Sun does it gravitationally.

Concept check

The Sun’s power is \(3.8\times10^{26}\ \mathrm{W}\). Its mass decrease per second is about

\( \Delta m = P/c^{2} = 3.8\times10^{26}/(3\times10^{8})^{2} \approx 4.2\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}} \). Distractors use \( c \) not \( c^{2} \).
