Section 27.3 extension

Mass–energy \( \Delta E = \Delta mc^{2} \)

Both fission and fusion weigh less after. The missing grams did not vanish — they became the fragments' kinetic energy. One relation weighs it: \( \Delta E = \Delta m\,c^{2} \).

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • state mass–energy relationship \( \Delta E = \Delta mc^{2} \) extension

  • use atomic mass unit as a unit of energy extension

  • determine the energy release in nuclear reactions extension

  • apply \( \Delta E = \Delta mc^{2} \) to solve problems extension

AThe missing-mass animation

Law

Energy and mass are equivalent: a mass defect \( \Delta m \) is convertible to energy

\[ \Delta E = \Delta m\,c^{2}, \quad c=3.00\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} \]

Note \( \Delta m \) is lost mass, not total mass. 1 g lost → \( 9\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{J} \) — enough for a town for a day. Fission/fusion/α decay all obey it.

  • Before you had rest mass + a little KE; after you have rest mass of products + much more KE (heat, light). Total mass+energy conserved; rest mass alone is not.
  • The KE after comes from Coulomb repulsion of fragments (fission) or from falling into the strong-force well (fusion). \( \Delta m c^{2} \) is the bookkeeping that predicts how large that KE must be without tracking every force.

Concept check

Where does the released energy mainly come from?

C. The neutron's KE is eV-scale; the output is MeV-scale. It is the rest-mass difference timed by \(c^{2}\).

Concept check

Which can be a unit of energy?

  1. atomic mass unit \( \mathrm{u} \)
  2. electronvolt \( \mathrm{eV} \)
  3. becquerel \( \mathrm{Bq} \)

B. Via \(E=mc^{2}\), mass unit \( \mathrm{u} \) × \(c^{2}\) is energy (931 MeV). Bq is activity (decays per second).

CWorking with \( \Delta m \) — three ways you will be asked

Recipe

1. Sum masses before, sum after (in \( \mathrm{u} \)). 2. \( \Delta m = \sum_{\text{before}} - \sum_{\text{after}} \). 3. \( \Delta E = \Delta m \times 931\ \mathrm{MeV} \) (or \( \Delta m\text{(kg)}\times c^{2} \)). 4. For many nuclei multiply by \( N = m_{\text{sample}}/m_{\text{one}} = (m/M)N_{A} \).

Worked example · Fusion D–T

\( {}^{2}\mathrm{H}\;(2.0136) + {}^{3}\mathrm{H}\;(3.0155) \rightarrow {}^{4}\mathrm{He}\;(4.0015) + \mathrm{n}\;(1.0087) \)

  1. \( \Delta m = (2.0136+3.0155)-(4.0015+1.0087)=0.0189\ \mathrm{u} \)
  2. \( \Delta E =0.0189\times931 = 17.6\ \mathrm{MeV}=2.82\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{J} \) per reaction
  3. Mass per reaction \( = (2.0136+3.0155)\times1.661\times10^{-27}=8.35\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg} \)
  4. Reactions in 5 g (equal D,T): \( N=0.005/8.35\times10^{-27}=5.99\times10^{23} \) → total ≈ \( 1.7\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{J} \)
\[ 0.0189\ \mathrm{u} \rightarrow 17.6\ \mathrm{MeV} \rightarrow 2.8\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{J} \]
Worked example · Fission \( {}^{235}\mathrm{U} \rightarrow {}^{148}\mathrm{La}+{}^{85}\mathrm{Br}+3\mathrm{n} \)

Given \( {}^{235}\mathrm{U}\;234.9934,\ \mathrm{La}\;147.9009,\ \mathrm{Br}\;84.8964\ \mathrm{u} \); reaction energy \(166.37\ \mathrm{MeV}\).

  1. Balance \( A: 235+1=148+85+x \Rightarrow x=3 \)
  2. \( \Delta m =166.37/931=0.1787\ \mathrm{u} \)
  3. Solve \( 234.9934+m_{\mathrm{n}} =147.9009+84.8964+3m_{\mathrm{n}}+0.1787 \Rightarrow m_{\mathrm{n}}=1.0087\ \mathrm{u} \)
  4. 5 g/s → \( N=0.005/(234.9934\times1.661\times10^{-27})=1.28\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}} \) → power \(166.37\mathrm{MeV}\times1.60\times10^{-13}\times N \approx 3.4\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{W}\)
Worked example · Coal vs uranium

U-235 fission converts 0.08% of mass. 1 kg U → \( \Delta m =0.0008\ \mathrm{kg} \) → \( \Delta E=0.0008c^{2}=7.2\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{J} \). Coal 1 kg →\(2.8\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{J}\) → coal needed \(7.2\times10^{13}/2.8\times10^{7}=2.6\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{kg} \).
