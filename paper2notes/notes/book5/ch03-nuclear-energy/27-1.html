Section 27.1

Fission and the chain reaction

A heavy nucleus can be made to split. The split needs a slow neutron, gives a spray of neutrons, and branches. Hold the branch at one and you have a reactor; let it run away and you have a bomb — with very different fuel.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • realise the release of energy in nuclear fission

  • realise nuclear chain reaction

AWhat fission is — and what makes it go

Definition

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into two lighter nuclei (fission fragments) when struck by a slow neutron. Energy is released, most as kinetic energy of the fragments. If the neutron is too fast it bounces off and fission does not occur — hence the moderator in a reactor.

\[ {}^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U} + {}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n} \rightarrow {}^{141}_{56}\mathrm{Ba} + {}^{92}_{36}\mathrm{Kr} + 3\,{}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n} + \text{energy} \]
  • Many splits are possible: \( {}^{235}\mathrm{U} + \mathrm{n} \rightarrow {}^{133}_{54}\mathrm{Xe} + {}^{101}_{38}\mathrm{Sr} + 2\mathrm{n} \); \( {}^{235}\mathrm{U} + \mathrm{n} \rightarrow {}^{148}_{57}\mathrm{La} + {}^{85}_{35}\mathrm{Br} + 3\mathrm{n} \) etc. Balances \( A \) and \( Z \) — same rule as decay, but products vary.
  • Energy per fission \( \approx 200\ \mathrm{MeV} \approx 3\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{J} \) (see 27.3 for the missing-mass calculation). One kilogram fully fissioned ≈ \( 7\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{J} \) — see Example 27.2.
  • Slow means thermal (\( \sim 0.025\ \mathrm{eV} \)): speed \( \sim 2000\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} \). Fast fission neutrons emerge at \( \sim10^{7}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} \) and must be slowed to trigger another U-235.

Concept check

Which correctly describes fission?

B. A is fusion, C is β decay, D is decay — fission is induced, not spontaneous or half-life governed.

Exam tip

If a question says "spontaneous, random, half-life" it is describing decay, not fission. If it says "needs ≥1 neutron per fission to sustain" it is describing chain reaction, not decay constant.

Concept check

(a) Fission occurs spontaneously at a rate set by a half-life.

False: fission is induced by neutron capture. Spontaneous random decay with a fixed \( T_{1/2} \) describes α (or β) decay.

(b) α decay of a nuclide always gives the same products; fission of U-235 can give different fragment pairs.

Actually true: the statement is correct — α decay products are fixed, fission products vary. The item as worded is true, so marking false tests misreading. (In mark scheme: true).

(c) The rate of fission can be controlled; the rate of α decay cannot.

True: control rods and moderator control fission; decay constant \( k=\ln2/T_{1/2} \) is intrinsic.

CBranching chain — when it lives and when it dies

Packing
  • Critical mass: smallest mass (for given density/purity) where \( k =1 \) on average. Depends on mass, density and purity of U-235. Natural uranium 0.7% U-235 is far below critical; bomb needs >90% enrichment and rapid compression (Little Boy used chemical explosives to bring bullet and target together).
  • If pieces are separate, neutrons born in one piece rarely reach the other — chain stops even though each piece alone plus together would be supercritical if reassembled. This is why cutting a plutonium sphere into four and separating them kills the chain (QB PHY15031115) — not because half-life changes, but because neutrons escape.
  • Domino analogy: close dominos chain continues; spread them too far and the fall stops. Supermod not needed for exam — just that separation/kills branch.

Concept check

A sphere of plutonium sustains a chain reaction. It is cut into four and the pieces separated. The chain stops because

D. Geometry lets neutrons escape. Half-life is irrelevant; fission rate is controlled externally. Electrons play no role in triggering fission.

Exam tip

Why a reactor cannot explode like a bomb even if runaway: (1) enrichment only ~3% (not >90%), (2) geometry/water needs moderation — bomb needs compressed supercritical assembly. Both are in mark schemes.

Concept check

Which application uses controlled nuclear fission?

B. Controlled chain in a reactor drives a generator. Medical imaging uses X-rays or tracers (Ch.26), fusion bomb is uncontrolled fusion, α decay is not fission.
