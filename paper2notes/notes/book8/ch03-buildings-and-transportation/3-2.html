Section 3.2

Energy efficiency in transportation

Why electric and hybrid vehicles waste less energy than petrol and diesel ones, and why buses and trains beat cars.

AEngines and motors

  • Most vehicles burn petrol, diesel or LPG in an internal combustion engine; most of the chemical energy ends up as heat. Diesel vehicles also emit particulates and nitrogen oxides.
  • An electric vehicle runs a motor from a battery. It gives off no exhaust where it is used, and the battery-to-wheel conversion is far more efficient.
  • A battery is heavy (hundreds of kilograms) and stores far less energy per kilogram than fuel, so an electric car has a shorter range. Fast chargers refill it in about half an hour.

Common mistake A battery stores chemical energy, not electrical energy. It becomes electrical energy only as the battery discharges.

Quick check

Why does an electric car usually travel a shorter distance on a full charge than a petrol car on a full tank?

The motor is the more efficient converter (not A), and regenerative braking recovers energy (not D). The limit is how little energy a battery holds for its mass.

BRegenerative braking

Definition

In regenerative braking the motor works as a generator while the car slows, turning part of its kinetic energy into electrical energy that recharges the battery.

  • Speeding up: chemical (battery) → electrical → kinetic energy (motor drives the wheels).
  • Braking: kinetic → electrical (generator) → chemical (battery). An ordinary car turns all of its kinetic energy into heat in the brakes.

Quick check

Which describes the energy changes in regenerative braking?

The generator makes electrical energy from the car's KE, and the battery stores it as chemical energy. A and B describe speeding up; C has the last two steps reversed.

Worked example

A 1200 kg electric car brakes from 20 m s⁻¹ to rest, and 40% of its kinetic energy is returned to the battery. How much energy is recovered?

Show solution
  1. \( \text{KE} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(1200)(20)^2 = 2.4 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J} \).
  2. Recovered \( = 0.40 \times 2.4 \times 10^{5} = 9.6 \times 10^{4}\ \text{J} \).

About 96 kJ goes back into the battery.

CHybrid electric vehicles

Definition

A hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) has an engine and an electric motor with its own battery; a computer decides which to use.

  • The motor helps at high load, so a smaller engine will do, and less energy is wasted moving its parts and against its internal friction.
  • The battery is charged by the engine and by regenerative braking, so an HEV never needs plugging in; all its energy comes from fuel.
  • Hybrid buses in Hong Kong use the engine only to drive a generator, which charges the battery and powers the motor; the engine can stop in stop-start traffic.

Quick check

True or false?

A hybrid electric vehicle gives off no exhaust.

It still burns fuel in its engine; it simply burns less of it.

The battery of a hybrid is usually much smaller than that of an electric car.

It only has to help the engine and store braking energy, not drive the whole journey.

DMass transport

  • A double-decker bus is about ten times as massive as a car but carries dozens of times as many people, so the energy used per passenger is far smaller.
  • Fewer vehicles also means less exhaust and less congestion.
  • Electric trains and trams give off nothing where they run, and a power station converts fuel to useful energy more efficiently than a vehicle engine does.
  • Energy per passenger depends on how full the vehicle is: a crowded train at rush hour uses much less per person than a near-empty one.

Quick check

A train uses more energy per passenger-kilometre off-peak than at rush hour. Why?

Most of the energy moves the heavy train itself, whoever is on board. Fewer passengers means a bigger share each.

EThe power needed to drive

\[ f_r = k_r N \qquad f_a = k_a v^2 \qquad P = (f_r + f_a)\,v \]
  • \( f_r \): rolling friction on the tyres, set by the tyres and the normal reaction \( N \), and independent of speed. \( f_a \): air resistance, set by the car's shape and size, \( \propto v^2 \).
  • At a steady speed the engine or motor must supply \( P = Fv \), and more again for its own losses and the air conditioner.
  • Hybrids help most in town: at low speed and in stop-start traffic the motor drives and braking energy is recovered.

Quick check

For the car in Fig. 3.8 (\( f_r = 300 \) N, \( k_a = 0.40 \) N s² m⁻²), the speed rises from 15 m s⁻¹ to 30 m s⁻¹. The power needed becomes about

\( P = (300 + 0.4v^2)v \): 5850 W at 15 m s⁻¹ and 19 800 W at 30 m s⁻¹, about 3.4 times. 8 times would be true only with air resistance alone (\( P \propto v^3 \)).
