Cooking without fire

Three electric cookers that each heat food a different way, and how to measure which one wastes least.

AElectric hotplate

\[ P = I^2 R = \frac{V^2}{R} \]
  • A current \( I \) through a heating element of resistance \( R \) gives heat at a rate \( P \). The hot element heats the plate, and heat conducts into the pot and food.
  • An electric oven uses the same kind of element; it heats the food by convection and radiation.
  • A good hotplate reaches about 70%. The very hot plate loses heat to the room by convection and radiation, and stays hot after it is switched off.

Quick check

True or false?

On the same mains voltage, a hotplate with a higher power rating has an element of higher resistance.

\( P = V^2/R \) with \( V \) fixed: a higher power needs a lower resistance.

A hotplate would also work on a dc supply.

The heating effect of a current does not depend on its direction, so dc heats the element too. An induction cooker, by contrast, needs a changing current.

BInduction cooker

  1. A high-frequency ac (about 25 kHz) flows in a flat coil under the cooking surface.
  2. It makes a magnetic field that changes rapidly through the base of the pot.
  3. The changing field induces eddy currents in the metal base; their heating effect heats the pot, and the pot cooks the food.
  • The pot must conduct electricity. A glass or ceramic dish has no eddy currents and stays cold.
  • Efficiency is above 80%, because the heat is made in the pot itself. The main loss is heat in the cooker's own circuit, which a fan carries away.
Common mistake The cooking surface does not make heat. It only gets warm from the hot pot sitting on it.

Quick check

Which of these cannot be heated on an induction cooker?

Glass does not conduct electricity, so no eddy currents flow in it and it is not heated. The metal pots all carry eddy currents.

CMicrowave oven

Definition

A water molecule is polar: its oxygen end is slightly negative and its hydrogen end slightly positive, so an electric field can turn it.

  • Microwaves of 2.45 GHz (wavelength \( c/f = 12.2 \) cm) have an electric field that reverses billions of times a second. Polar water molecules flip back and forth with it, and their energy becomes internal energy of the food.
  • The waves reach a few centimetres into the food, so it heats throughout rather than only at the surface. A turntable evens out the heating.
  • It suits food rich in water; the dish is not heated directly. Efficiency is about 60%: most of the waste is heat in the circuit that makes the microwaves.

Quick check

True or false?

A microwave oven heats food by inducing eddy currents in it.

That is the induction cooker. A microwave oven makes polar water molecules flip in an oscillating electric field.

An electric cooker warms the kitchen far less than a gas stove does.

A gas flame transfers only about 40% of its energy to the pot; most of the rest heats the room. Electric cookers waste much less.

\[ \eta = \frac{mc\,\Delta T}{P\,t} \times 100\% \]
  • To compare cookers, heat the same mass \( m \) of water through the same \( \Delta T \) on each and time it; \( c = 4200\ \text{J kg}^{-1}\,^\circ\text{C}^{-1} \) for water.
  • Real efficiencies depend on the pot, the food and how the cooker is used.

Quick check

A hotplate (40% efficient) and an induction cooker (90% efficient) have the same power rating. The hotplate takes 9 minutes to boil some water. How long does the induction cooker take for the same water?

The water needs the same energy, and the useful power is \( \eta P \), so time \( \propto 1/\eta \): \( 9 \times 0.40/0.90 = 4 \) min. 20 min inverts the ratio.

Worked example

1.5 kg of water is heated from 20 °C to 90 °C. A 1500 W hotplate takes 480 s; an 1800 W induction cooker takes 290 s. Find each efficiency.

Show solution
  1. Energy to the water: \( mc\Delta T = 1.5 \times 4200 \times 70 = 4.41 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J} \).
  2. Hotplate: \( Pt = 1500 \times 480 = 7.20 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J} \), so \( \eta = 61\% \).
  3. Induction: \( Pt = 1800 \times 290 = 5.22 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J} \), so \( \eta = 84\% \).

Hotplate 61%, induction cooker 84%: the induction cooker wastes less.
