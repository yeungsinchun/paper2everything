Section 2.2

Moving heat around

An air conditioner does work to move heat out of a cool room into the hot air outside.

What to remember

\( Q_H = Q_C + W \)heat dumped outside = heat taken from the room + work done

Cooling capacity \( = Q_C / t \)heat removed per second, in W or kW

\( \text{COP} = \dfrac{Q_C}{W} \)cooling capacity ÷ input power; no unit, usually above 1

Refrigerantevaporates indoors (absorbs latent heat), condenses outdoors (releases it)

AA heat pump works against the natural flow

\[ Q_H = Q_C + W \]
  • Heat flows naturally from hot to cold. A heat pump moves heat the other way, from a cold region to a hot one, and needs work to do it.
  • \( Q_C \): heat removed from the cold room; \( W \): work done (electrical energy used); \( Q_H \): heat given to the hot outdoors. Energy is conserved, so \( Q_H \) is the largest.
  • An air conditioner and a refrigerator are both heat pumps.

Quick check

An air conditioner with a COP of 4 gives \( 1.0 \times 10^{7} \) J of heat to the outdoors in one hour. How much heat does it remove from the room?

\( Q_C = 4W \) and \( Q_H = Q_C + W = 5W \), so \( W = 2.0 \times 10^{6} \) J and \( Q_C = 8.0 \times 10^{6} \) J. A is the work, B divides \( Q_H \) by the COP, and D forgets the work.

BCooling capacity and COP

Definition

A unit's cooling capacity tells how quickly it transfers heat out of the room; power gives it in watts or kilowatts.

Definition

The coefficient of performance (COP) is the heat removed divided by the work done to remove it.

\[ \begin{gathered} \text{cooling capacity} = \frac{Q_C}{t} \\ \text{COP} = \frac{Q_C}{W} = \frac{\text{cooling capacity}}{\text{input electrical power}} \end{gathered} \]
Common mistake COP is not an efficiency. It has no unit and is usually greater than 1: a COP of 3 moves 3 J of heat for every 1 J of electrical energy.

Quick check

A cooling unit removes heat at 2.0 kW while using a COP of 2.5. Calculate its electrical input power.

Input power = cooling capacity ÷ COP = 2.0 / 2.5 = 0.8 kW. 5.0 kW multiplies instead; 2.8 kW is the heat given out outdoors per second.

Worked example

A bedroom air conditioner has a cooling capacity of 2.6 kW and a COP of 3.2. It runs 8 hours a day for 30 days at $1.10 per kW h. Find the cost.

Show solution
  1. Input power \( = 2.6 / 3.2 = 0.8125\ \text{kW} \).
  2. Energy \( = 0.8125 \times 8 \times 30 = 195\ \text{kW h} \).
  3. Cost \( = 195 \times 1.10 = \$214.50 \).

About $215 for the month.

Worked example

A room 4 m × 5 m × 2.8 m holds air of density 1.2 kg m⁻³ and specific heat capacity 1000 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹. Estimate how long a 2.0 kW air conditioner takes to cool the air by 6 °C, and say why the real time is longer.

Show solution
  1. Mass of air \( = 1.2 \times 56 = 67.2\ \text{kg} \).
  2. Heat to remove \( = mc\Delta T = 67.2 \times 1000 \times 6 = 4.03 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J} \).
  3. Time \( = 4.03 \times 10^{5} / 2000 \approx 200\ \text{s} \).

About 200 s. In practice the walls, furniture and people must be cooled too, and heat keeps leaking in, so it takes longer.

CThe refrigeration cycle

PartWhat happens to the refrigerantHeat
Evaporator (indoors)Cold liquid evaporates in coiled tubesAbsorbs latent heat from the room air
CompressorGas is compressed to high pressure and high temperatureWork is done on it (the electrical input)
Condenser (outdoors)Hot gas condenses back to liquidReleases latent heat to the outdoor air
Expansion valveLiquid expands to low pressure and low temperatureReady to absorb heat again
  • In a split-type unit the indoor part is the evaporator; the compressor, condenser and expansion valve are outdoors.
  • The condenser has metal (copper or aluminium) tubes, many thin fins and a fan, all to pass heat to the air quickly.
  • A refrigerator runs the same cycle; reversing the flow turns an air conditioner into a heater.

Quick check

An air conditioner is cooling a room on a hot day. Put these in increasing order of temperature: refrigerant in the evaporator (\( T_e \)), refrigerant in the condenser (\( T_c \)), outdoor air (\( T_o \)).

The evaporator must be colder than the room to take heat in, so it is coldest. The condenser must be hotter than the outdoor air to give heat out, so it is hottest.

DCentral air-conditioning

  • The air handling unit (AHU) cools the air and sends it through ducts to the rooms. It can also control humidity and filter the air.
  • Chilled water flows between the AHU and a chiller; a second water loop takes the heat from the chiller to cooling towers.
  • Water is used because its high specific heat capacity lets it carry a lot of heat with a small temperature change. These loops involve no change of state.
  • Waste heat can be reclaimed, for example to preheat bath water or to reheat air after dehumidification.

Quick check

True or false?

Water suits the cooling loops because a large amount of heat changes its temperature only a little.

That is what a high specific heat capacity means.

The chilled-water loop works by evaporating and condensing the water.

The water stays liquid; only its temperature changes. Changes of state happen in the refrigeration cycle inside the chiller.

EEnergy labels for air conditioners

GradeEnergy consumption index \( I_c \)
1\( I_c \le 85\% \)
2\( 85\% < I_c \le 95\% \)
3\( 95\% < I_c \le 105\% \)
4\( 105\% < I_c \le 120\% \)
5\( I_c > 120\% \)
\[ I_c = \frac{E}{E_\text{avg}} \times 100\% \]
  • \( E \): the model's energy use in a standard cooling test; \( E_\text{avg} \): the average for models of the same type and similar capacity. Lower is better.
  • In Hong Kong the label's annual energy consumption assumes 1200 hours of use a year, so average input power = annual consumption ÷ 1200 h.
  • With the cooling capacity also on the label, \( \text{COP} = \text{capacity} / \text{input power} \).

Quick check

For one type of air conditioner, \( E_\text{avg} = 1000 \) kW h. A model uses 900 kW h in the test. What grade does it get?

\( I_c = 900/1000 = 90\% \), which lies between 85% and 95%: grade 2. Grade 1 needs 850 kW h or less.

Worked example

A label shows an annual energy consumption of 1320 kW h and a cooling capacity of 3.3 kW. Find the COP, and the cost of running it 6 hours a day for 120 days at $1.20 per kW h.

Show solution
  1. Input power \( = 1320 / 1200 = 1.1\ \text{kW} \); \( \text{COP} = 3.3 / 1.1 = 3.0 \).
  2. Hours \( = 6 \times 120 = 720\ \text{h} \); energy \( = 1.1 \times 720 = 792\ \text{kW h} \).
  3. Cost \( = 792 \times 1.20 = \$950.40 \).

COP 3.0; about $950.
