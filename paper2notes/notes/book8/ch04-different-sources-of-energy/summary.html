Chapter 4

Summary: Different Sources of Energy

Formulas, definitions, every source side by side, and the traps.

1Formulas 2Definitions 3Sources compared 4Traps

Goals

After this chapter you can:

  • explain how a turbine and generator make electricity, and sort energy sources into renewable and non-renewable

  • use the binding energy curve and \( \Delta E = \Delta m c^2 \) to explain why fission and fusion release energy

  • describe a chain reaction and the jobs of the fuel rods, control rods, moderator and coolant in a fission reactor

  • estimate the power from wind, falling water and sunlight

  • explain how a photovoltaic cell gives a current

  • weigh the advantages and drawbacks of each source

  • describe the pollution energy use causes, energy storage, and the natural and enhanced greenhouse effect

2Definitions

Renewable source
Replaced naturally in a short time: solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, biomass
Non-renewable source
Used up far faster than it forms: fossil fuels, nuclear fuel
Binding energy
Energy needed to separate a nucleus into its protons and neutrons
Mass defect
Mass lost in a nuclear reaction; it appears as energy \( \Delta m c^2 \)
Chain reaction
Neutrons from one fission go on to cause further fissions
Moderator
Slows fast neutrons so U-235 can capture them
Control rod
Absorbs spare neutrons to control the rate of fission
Solar constant
Solar power per unit area facing the Sun at the top of the atmosphere
Photovoltaic cell
A p-n junction that turns light directly into a direct current
Greenhouse gas
A gas in the atmosphere that absorbs and re-emits infrared

4Traps that cost marks

Moderator vs control rod

The moderator slows neutrons and keeps the chain going. Control rods absorb neutrons and slow the chain.

Per nucleon

Stability follows \( E_b/A \), not total \( E_b \). Uranium has a larger total but a smaller value per nucleon than iron.

Swept area

In \( P = \tfrac12 \eta\rho A v^3 \), \( A = \pi r^2 \) with the blade length as \( r \).

Cube, not square

Double the wind speed and the power goes up 8 times, because the mass per second doubles too.

Top of the atmosphere

The solar constant is measured above the air, facing the Sun. At the ground, allow for losses and \( \cos\theta \).

Natural vs enhanced

The natural greenhouse effect keeps us 33 °C warmer. The enhanced effect from extra gases is what causes global warming.
