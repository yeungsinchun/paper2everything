Domestic electricity

AElectricity in the home

  • Electricity supplies more than 70% of the energy used in Hong Kong homes, because one supply can be turned into almost any form we need.
  • Each appliance has one useful output. Whatever else it produces, mostly heat in the appliance and its surroundings, is wasted.

Quick check

True or false?

The useful output of an electric fan is the kinetic energy of the moving air.

A fan exists to move air. The warm motor and the hum are wasted outputs.

All the electrical energy taken in by a kettle ends up in the water.

Some energy also warms the kettle body and escapes to the air, so less than 100% reaches the water.

BEnd-use energy efficiency

Definition

The end-use energy efficiency \( \eta \) of an appliance is the useful energy it gives out as a percentage of the electrical energy it takes in.

\[ \eta = \frac{E_\text{out}}{E_\text{in}} \times 100\% = \frac{P_\text{out}}{P_\text{in}} \times 100\% \]
  • \( E_\text{out} \): useful energy output (J); \( E_\text{in} \): electrical energy input (J). Dividing both by time gives the power form (W).
  • The filament bulb in Fig. 1.2 has \( \eta = 2\% \). A kettle or an induction cooker is far higher, because heat is its useful output.
Exam tip You pay for all of \( E_\text{in} \), not just the useful part. A low \( \eta \) means paying for energy you cannot use.

Quick check

A 1200 W kettle delivers 900 W to the water. What is its end-use energy efficiency?

\( \eta = 900 / 1200 \times 100\% = 75\% \). 25% is the share that is wasted. 133% puts the input on top; no appliance gives out more than it takes in. 300 W is the wasted power, not an efficiency.

Worked example

A 50 W ceiling fan gives the air kinetic energy at 32 W. It runs for 6 hours. Find its efficiency and the energy it wastes, in kW h.

Show solution
  1. Efficiency: \( \eta = 32 / 50 \times 100\% = 64\% \).
  2. Wasted power: \( 50 - 32 = 18\ \text{W} = 0.018\ \text{kW} \).
  3. Wasted energy: \( 0.018\ \text{kW} \times 6\ \text{h} = 0.108\ \text{kW h} \).

\( \eta = 64\% \); about 0.11 kW h is wasted.

COverall efficiency: every stage counts

\[ \eta_\text{overall} = \eta_\text{station} \times \eta_\text{transmission} \times \eta_\text{appliance} \]
  • A typical fossil-fuel station turns only about 40% of the fuel's chemical energy into electrical energy; about 5% more is lost in transmission.
  • So an induction cooker with \( \eta = 75\% \) uses only \( 0.40 \times 0.95 \times 0.75 = 28.5\% \) of the fuel's energy to heat food.
  • An appliance's label states only its end-use efficiency, the last stage of the chain.

Quick check

A power station is 45% efficient, transmission is 90% efficient and a cooker is 80% efficient. What fraction of the fuel's energy heats the food?

Multiply the stages: \( 0.45 \times 0.90 \times 0.80 = 0.324 \). 45% ignores two stages; 72% ignores the power station; 215% adds the percentages, which can never exceed 100%.

DReading an energy label

Label typeWhat it tells you
Recognition typeOnly that the model meets the minimum efficiency and performance standard
Grading typeA grade from 1 (most efficient) to 5 (least); the annual energy consumption under standard use, for comparing models of the same kind; performance data such as cooling capacity or efficacy
  • In Hong Kong the labels belong to the Energy Efficiency Labelling Scheme, run by the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department.
  • Annual cost = annual energy consumption (kW h) × price per kW h. Your own use differs from the standard hours on the label.

Quick check

A refrigerator label shows an annual energy consumption of 300 kW h. Electricity costs $1.20 per kW h. What does it cost to run for a year under standard use?

\( 300 \times 1.20 = \$360 \). $250 divides instead of multiplying; $300 forgets the price; $3600 slips a power of ten.

EEnergy-saving devices

DeviceWhat it doesWhy it saves energy
ThermostatCuts the power when the room or water reaches the set temperatureNo energy is spent going past the target
TimerSwitches an appliance off after a set timeNothing runs longer than needed
Motion sensorSwitches lights on only when someone is thereEmpty rooms stay dark
Standby modeDrops the appliance to a low-power state when idleIdle power falls, though switching off at the wall saves more
Common mistake Saving devices cut the time an appliance runs. They do not change its end-use efficiency. Rechargeable batteries cut waste, but they are not energy-saving devices.

Quick check

True or false?

Fitting a thermostat raises the end-use energy efficiency of an air conditioner.

The air conditioner converts energy exactly as before; the thermostat only stops it running once the room is cool enough.

An appliance on standby still uses some electrical energy.

Standby keeps a clock, a remote-control receiver or a display alive. Only switching off at the wall stops it completely.
