AElliptical orbits

Key idea

A planet follows an ellipse with the Sun at one focus. The semi-major axis a is half the longest diameter; perihelion and aphelion are nearest and farthest.

a = (rp + ra)/2. For a circle, the foci coincide.

Quick check

Where is the Sun in a general planetary ellipse?

It is at one focus, not generally at the centre.

BEqual areas in equal times

Key idea

The line from planet to Sun sweeps equal areas in equal time intervals. The planet moves faster near perihelion and slower near aphelion.

This is Kepler’s second law; it follows from angular momentum conservation under a central force.

Quick check

Where is the planet fastest?

At perihelion, where it is closest to the Sun.

CPeriod and orbit size

Key idea

For objects orbiting the same central mass, T²/a³ is constant. In solar units, T² = a³ when T is measured in years and a in AU.

A = 4 AU gives T = 8 years. The shortcut applies only to the Sun in these units.

Quick check

If a doubles, the period factor is?

T scales as a^(3/2), giving 2√2.

Worked example: Halley’s orbit

From orbit size to period

  1. Use the rounded perihelion and aphelion distances: a = (0.59 + 35.1)/2 = 17.845 AU.
  2. In solar units, T² = a³, so T = 17.8453/2 ≈ 75.4 years.

Halley’s period is about 75 years.
