Chapter summary

Starlight: messengers from the stars

The relationships and distinctions to recall before solving problems.

Formula sheet

d(pc) = 1/p(arcsec); L = 4πR²σT⁴; λmaxT = 2.90 × 10⁻³ m K; Δλ/λ = vr/c; v = H₀d.

Check units and conditions before substituting values.

Definitions

  • Parallax — half the apparent angular shift over six months.
  • Parsec — distance at which parallax is one arcsecond.
  • Luminosity — total power emitted by a star.
  • Apparent magnitude — brightness as seen from Earth.
  • Absolute magnitude — magnitude at a standard distance of 10 pc.
  • Red shift — spectral shift to longer wavelength.
  • Radial velocity — velocity component along the line of sight.

Key ideas

  • 4.1 · Distance and brightness of stars — Parallax, magnitude and the inverse-square law.
  • 4.2 · Classification of stars — Spectra, temperature, luminosity and the H–R diagram.
  • 4.3 · Doppler effect and its applications — Radial motion, binaries, dark matter and cosmic expansion.
Common mistakes

Smaller magnitude means brighter. A Doppler shift measures radial velocity, not total speed.
