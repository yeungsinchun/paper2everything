AAngular size

Key idea

For a small angle in radians, θ ≈ D/d, where D is diameter and d is distance. The same star looks smaller when farther away.

Convert degrees to radians before using the small-angle equation.

Quick check

If distance doubles at fixed diameter, angular size?

It halves because θ is proportional to 1/d.

BParallax and parsecs

Key idea

Parallax is half a nearby star’s apparent shift between opposite sides of Earth’s orbit. Distance in parsecs is d = 1/p when p is in arcseconds.

A star with p = 0.5 arcsec is 2 pc away; smaller parallax means greater distance.

Quick check

What distance corresponds to p = 0.25 arcsec?

d = 1/0.25 = 4 pc.

Worked example: Proxima Centauri

Parallax to distance

  1. For p = 0.77 arcsec, d = 1/p = 1/0.77 ≈ 1.30 pc.
  2. Convert to light years: 1.30 × 3.26 ≈ 4.24 ly.

The star is about 1.30 pc, or 4.24 ly, away.

CMagnitude and brightness

Key idea

Smaller magnitude means brighter. Five magnitudes correspond to a brightness factor of 100. Received intensity follows I = L/(4πd²).

Apparent magnitude is as seen from Earth; absolute magnitude compares stars at 10 pc.

Quick check

At twice the distance, intensity becomes?

Inverse-square spreading gives one quarter.
