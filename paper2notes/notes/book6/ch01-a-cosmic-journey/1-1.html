ASteps of 100 BAU, light year, parsec

AA journey in steps of 100

  • Space between the planets and between the stars is almost empty. The step that first shows another star is near 1017 m, about 7 million times the Earth–Sun distance.
  • Light from the nearest star, Proxima Centauri, takes about 4.2 years to reach us.
  • The Milky Way is about 1021 m (about 105 light years) across and holds more than 1011 stars.
  • Human travel is tiny on this ladder: astronauts reached the Moon (3.8 × 108 m) in about 3 days; the most distant probe, Voyager 1, has gone about 2 × 1013 m in nearly 50 years.

Definition

An astronomical unit (AU) is Earth’s average orbital radius: 1 AU = 1.496 × 1011 m. It suits distances inside the solar system.

Definition

One light year (ly) is the distance light travels in a vacuum in one year. It suits distances between stars and galaxies.

\[ \begin{aligned} 1\ \text{ly} &= c \times (1\ \text{year}) \\ &= (2.998 \times 10^{8})(365.25 \times 24 \times 3600)\ \mathrm{m} \\ &= 9.461 \times 10^{15}\ \mathrm{m} \end{aligned} \]

Common mistake

A light year measures distance, not time. "The star is 25 light years away" means its light left it 25 years ago.

Quick check

(a) The light year is a unit of time.

False. It is the distance light covers in one year, 9.46 × 1015 m.

(b) Sunlight we see now left the Sun about 8 minutes ago.

True. 1 AU ÷ c = 1.496 × 1011 m ÷ 3.00 × 108 m s−1 ≈ 499 s ≈ 8.3 min.

(c) One parsec is shorter than one light year.

False. 1 pc = 3.26 ly, so the parsec is the longer unit.

Quick check

Arrange these lengths from shortest to longest: 1000 km, 0.1 AU, 10 light-minutes.

A. In metres: 1000 km = 1.0 × 106 m; 0.1 AU = 1.5 × 1010 m; 10 light-minutes = 10 × 60 × 3.0 × 108 = 1.8 × 1011 m. B treats 0.1 AU as tiny because the number is small. C and D forget that 1 AU is itself 8.3 light-minutes, so 10 light-minutes is more than 1 AU.

Worked example: compare stellar and planetary distances

Worked example

A star lies 12 ly away. A planet orbits its star at 8 AU. Express the star distance in metres, then estimate the sunlight travel time to that planet.

  1. Convert the stellar distance: \(12(9.461 \times 10^{15}) = 1.14 \times 10^{17}\ \mathrm{m}\).
  2. Convert the planet’s orbit: \(8(1.496 \times 10^{11}) = 1.20 \times 10^{12}\ \mathrm{m}\).
  3. Light time is distance divided by light speed: \(t = (1.20 \times 10^{12})/(3.00 \times 10^8) = 4.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{s}\).

The star is about \(1.14 \times 10^{17}\) m away; its planet receives starlight after about 67 minutes.
