APlanets and small bodies

Definition

A planet orbits a star and shines mainly by reflected light. A natural satellite orbits a planet. A comet contains ice and dust; sunlight and the solar wind push its tail away from the Sun.

TerrestrialJovian
Small, rocky, thin atmospheresLarge, mostly hydrogen and helium
Few or no moonsMany moons; ring systems

Quick check

Which statement is true?

A satellite orbits a planet. Planets mostly reflect starlight, and comet tails point away from the Sun.

BStars and nebulae

Definition

A star is a luminous sphere powered by nuclear fusion. A nebula is interstellar gas and dust; gravity can gather its dense regions into stars.

A star cluster is a group of stars held together by gravity. The stars can form from the same nebula.

Quick check

What can contract to form stars?

Gravity can contract dense gas and dust in a nebula.

CGalaxies and galaxy clusters

Definition

A galaxy is a huge system of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity. Galaxies form groups and clusters; the Milky Way belongs to the Local Group.

The Local Group’s two largest members are the Milky Way and Andromeda, about 2.5 million light years apart.

Quick check

Is every naked-eye star in the Milky Way?

Andromeda is a separate galaxy visible to the naked eye under dark skies.

DSuperclusters, filaments and voids

Clusters group galaxies; superclusters group clusters. On the largest scales, filaments surround vast voids.

Quick check

Choose the increasing size order.

A galaxy contains star clusters; galaxy clusters contain many galaxies.
