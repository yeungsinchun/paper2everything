Section 2.1

Apparent motion in the sky

Models explain apparent motion and the evidence that changed astronomy.

Remember

Core ideaThe sky appears to turn westward once a day because Earth rotates eastward in about 24 hours. Star trails record this apparent motion.

FormulaThe celestial sphere is a useful direction model, not a physical shell.

AvoidSeparate the model’s appearance from the physical cause.

ADaily motionBYearly motion and the eclipticCRetrograde motion

ADaily motion

Key idea

The sky appears to turn westward once a day because Earth rotates eastward in about 24 hours. Star trails record this apparent motion.

The celestial sphere is a useful direction model, not a physical shell.

Quick check

What causes the daily sweep of stars?

Earth’s rotation causes the daily apparent sweep.

BYearly motion and the ecliptic

Key idea

Earth’s orbit makes the Sun appear to move eastward against the stars. Its apparent yearly path is the ecliptic.

The constellation near the Sun is in the daytime sky; the opposite constellation is visible near midnight.

Quick check

When the Sun is in front of Sagittarius, is Sagittarius a midnight constellation?

No. Sagittarius is near the Sun in the daytime sky.

CRetrograde motion

Key idea

A planet can appear to move westward against the stars for a short time, then resume eastward motion. This is a viewing effect as planets move in their orbits.

Mercury and Venus remain near the Sun in the sky and appear as morning or evening objects.

Quick check

Can Venus appear opposite the Sun at midnight?

No. Its orbit inside Earth’s orbit limits its apparent separation from the Sun.
