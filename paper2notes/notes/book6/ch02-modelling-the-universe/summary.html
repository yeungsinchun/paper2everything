Chapter summary

Modelling the universe

The relationships and distinctions to recall before solving problems.

Formula sheet

sin θ = rplanet / rEarth (maximum elongation of an inner planet).

Check units and conditions before substituting values.

Definitions

  • Daily motion — apparent daily turning of the sky.
  • Ecliptic — apparent yearly path of the Sun against the stars.
  • Retrograde motion — temporary apparent westward motion of a planet.
  • Geocentric — Earth-centred model.
  • Heliocentric — Sun-centred model.
  • Epicycle — small circle carrying a planet in a Ptolemaic model.
  • Deferent — large circle carrying an epicycle’s centre.

Key ideas

  • 2.1 · Apparent motion in the sky — Daily and yearly motion, retrograde motion and inferior planets.
  • 2.2 · Astronomy of the ancient Greeks — Geocentric spheres, deferents and epicycles.
  • 2.3 · The Copernican revolution — Heliocentric motion and the observations that changed astronomy.
Common mistakes

Daily sky motion comes from Earth’s rotation. Retrograde motion is apparent, not a reversal of orbital motion.
