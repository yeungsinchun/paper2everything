Section 24.4

Transformers and high-voltage transmission

AIdeal transformer

Key idea

Transformer: two coils on common iron core (laminated). Alternating flux links both. Ideal: \(\dfrac{V_P}{V_S}=\dfrac{N_P}{N_S}\), \(I_P V_P \approx I_S V_S\) (power conserved), so \(I_S/I_P = N_P/N_S\) inverse. Step-up: NS>NP; step-down: NS

Key ideaDetail
EfficiencyReal <100%: copper (I^2R), hysteresis, eddy, flux leakage. Laminated soft iron, thick low-R wire, close coupling improve.
ExampleNS=2×NP → VS=2×VP, IS≈IP/2.

Quick check

A transformer NP=200, NS=100 steps down 220 V to?

VS=VP×NS/NP=110 V. D would be zero for d.c. input because no changing flux.

BGrid: why step high

Key idea

High-voltage transmission: power \(P = V I\), line loss \(P_{\rm loss}=I^2 R_{\rm line}\). For fixed delivered power, higher V → lower I → much lower loss. So generated (~tens kV) stepped up to ~100s kV for long lines, then stepped down near homes (~220 V).

Key ideaDetail
AdvantageHigher V: same P at lower I → I^2R down. Also thinner cables possible.
StagesPower station → step-up → transmission → step-down → distribution → consumer.

Quick check

Why transmit at high voltage?

P=VI fixes I=P/V; loss I^2R ∝1/V^2. Higher V slashes waste heating.
