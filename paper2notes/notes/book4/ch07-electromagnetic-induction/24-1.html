Section 24.1

Flux, Lenz's law and Faraday's law

Change of flux drives an e.m.f.; Lenz says which way.

AMagnetic flux

Key idea

Flux \(\Phi = B A \cos\theta\), \(\theta\) between B and normal to area. Unit weber (Wb) = T·m^2. B = flux density (flux per unit area normal). N turns: linkage \(N\Phi\). Moving rod length l speed v perpendicular to B: motional \(\varepsilon = B l v\).

Key ideaDetail
Angleθ=0° (B∥normal) → max; θ=90° → zero.
Weber1 Wb is 1 T through 1 m^2 normal.

Concept check

A 0.1 m^2 loop normal parallel to 0.2 T. Flux is

BA cos0 =0.2*0.1=0.02 Wb. Tilt 60° → 0.01 Wb.

BLenz and Faraday

Key idea

Faraday (magnitude average): \(\bar{\varepsilon} = -N \Delta\Phi/\Delta t\). Sign = Lenz: induced B/current opposes the change that produced it (energy conservation). Faster change or more turns → larger ε. Search coil: \(\int \varepsilon dt = N\Delta\Phi\) so area under ε–t gives flux change, and with known A gives B.

Key ideaDetail
Lenz trickIf flux into page increases, induced current makes B out of page (anti-clockwise when viewed).
Graph slopeΦ–t slope = ε/N; ε–t area = Φ change.

Concept check

Magnet north pole approaches a loop from above. Induced current viewed from above is

Flux into loop (down) increases; induced makes flux up → field out of page → anti-clockwise current (right-hand grip). Speed affects magnitude, not direction.
