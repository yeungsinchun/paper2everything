Section 20.2

Electric field and field lines

A charge modifies space around it. The field tells a test charge what force it will feel, and field lines picture that.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • describe the electric field around a point charge and between parallel charged plates

  • represent an electric field using field lines and explain how charges interact via a field

  • define \(E = F/q\) for a positive test charge and state \(E = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}\) and \(E = \dfrac{V}{d}\)

AField strength as force per unit charge

Key idea

Electric field strength at a point is \(E = F/q\) (vector) for a small positive test charge \(q\) placed there. Around an isolated point charge \(Q\): \(E = \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}\) radially outward if \(Q>0\), inward if \(Q<0\). The field exists whether a test charge is there or not.

Key ideaDetail
Units\([E]=\mathrm{N\,C^{-1}}=\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\).
SuperpositionFields add as vectors; on the bisector of two \(+Q\) the horizontal components cancel.
Test charge signDefined for \(+\). Electron feels \(F = qE\) opposite to \(E\).

Concept check

Where can \(E=0\) for a fixed \(-4Q\) at \(x=0\) and \(+Q\) at \(x=L\) on the line beyond them?

Set \(4Q/r_1^2 = Q/r_2^2\). The weaker charge wins only when much closer. Zero lies outside, on the side of the smaller charge (+Q, right). Between, fields point the same way, cannot cancel.

BUniform field between plates

Key idea

Between large, close, parallel plates with p.d. \(V\) and separation \(d\): \(E = V/d\) uniform (same vector everywhere). A charge between them feels constant \(F = qE\). For an electron: accelerates toward the positive plate; kinematics with \(a = qE/m\) is constant, so projectile-like if entering sideways.

Key ideaDetail
UniformLines parallel, equally spaced. Charge projected perpendicular to \(E\) traces a parabola.
PotentialStepping along \(E\) loses potential: \(V = E d\). Earthed plate \(=0\).
Units check\(V/d = (\mathrm{J/C})/\mathrm{m}=\mathrm{N/C}\), consistent.

Concept check

An electron enters a uniform \(E\) region (plates) horizontally with speed \(v\). Its path is

Constant vertical force \(qE\) (opposite to E for negative). Horizontal velocity constant, vertical acceleration constant → parabola. Toward + plate because E points − to +, electron is −.
