Section 20.1

Charge and Coulomb's law

ATwo charges, one carrier

Key idea

Two kinds of charge, labelled positive and negative. Like charges repel ( \(+/+\) or \(-/-\) push apart). Opposite charges attract ( \(+/-\) pull together). The carrier is the electron: adding electrons makes a surface negative, removing them leaves it positive. An isolated metal sphere charged by induction ends up net charged without contact.

Key ideaDetail
EvidenceGlass rod rubbed with silk attracts a + test foil; ebonite rubbed with fur repels it - same apparatus, opposite charge.
Charging by frictionElectrons move from the material that holds them less tightly to the one that holds them more tightly.
QuantizationCharge comes in units of \(e = 1.6\times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}\).

Quick check

A glass rod is rubbed with silk and becomes positively charged. Which statement is correct?

Charging is electron transfer. Glass loses electrons to silk, so glass is left positive. No protons move, no new charge is created.

BCoulomb's law for point charges

Key idea

For point charges (size \(\ll r\)), the magnitude of the electrostatic force is \(F = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{|Q_1 Q_2|}{r^2}\) with \(1/(4\pi\varepsilon_0)=8.99\times10^9\ \mathrm{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}\). Direction is along the line of centres: repulsive for like, attractive for opposite. Resultant is the vector sum; a test charge between two others feels zero where forces cancel.

Key ideaDetail
Proportionalities\(F \propto Q_1 Q_2\) (doubling either charge doubles \(F\)). \(F \propto 1/r^2\) (halving \(r\) quadruples \(F\)).
SuperpositionThree collinear charges: \(F_{\rm on\,2}=F_{12}+F_{32}\) with signs for direction.
Units\(\varepsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{C^2\,N^{-1}\,m^{-2}}\). For order: two \(1\ \mu\mathrm{C}\) at \(10\ \mathrm{cm}\) repel with ~0.9 N.

Quick check

Two identical metal spheres each carry \( +6Q\). They touch and then separate to the same distance as before. How does the repulsion compare?

Identical spheres share charge equally. Each had +6Q, total +12Q → each +6Q after. Same charge and same distance → same force. If one had been different, e.g., +6Q and -2Q → total +4Q → +2Q each, force would drop.
