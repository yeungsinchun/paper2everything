Section 22.2

Domestic wiring and safety

Why sockets are parallel and how fuses/cables stay safe.

AHousehold circuit

Key idea

Sockets in parallel: each gets full mains V, can be switched independently; wiring live (brown) carries 220 V, neutral (blue) near 0, earth (green/yellow) safety to case. Parallel: one appliance failure doesn't kill others; series would share V incorrectly.

Key ideaDetail
Parallel vs seriesParallel: V same, I adds, independent. Series would dim/overvolt.

Concept check

Why domestic sockets are NOT in series?

Series divides V and fails together. Parallel gives each full V and independent control.

BFuse and cable choice

Key idea

Operating current \(I = P/V_{rated}\). Fuse chosen: rating > operating I (so normal not blown) but < cable safe max, and as low as practical. Fuses/MCB on live, not neutral, so fault breaks high potential; double-insulated appliances may omit earth. Cable cross-section sets max continuous I.

Key ideaDetail
Example1 kW at 220 V → ~4.5 A → choose 5 A fuse, not 13 A; cable 6 A rating okay.

Concept check

A 2 kW heater at 220 V should use

I≈9.1 A → 13 A fuse on live reasonable; neutral/earth fuses don't break live fault. 3 A would blow immediately.
