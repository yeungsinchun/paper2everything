Section 22.2

Domestic wiring and safety

AHousehold circuit

State

Parallel branches: 220 V each; earth bonds the metal case.

Live · brown Neutral · blue Earth · green/yellow Cable colours identify conductors, not quantities.
Key idea
Live
Brown. Carries the 220 V; switches and fuses go here.
Neutral
Blue. Completes the circuit back to the supply, near 0 V.
Earth
Green/yellow. Bonded to a metal case as a safety path.

Sockets are wired in parallel: every branch gets the full 220 V and can be switched on its own.

Quick check

Why are sockets wired in parallel, not in series?

Parallel gives every branch the full 220 V and independent switching; one branch fault does not stop the others.

BFuse and cable choice

Working current: what the load draws at 220 V.

Key idea
Working current
\(I = P/V\) at the rated 220 V.
Fuse
The lowest standard rating above the working current.
Cable
Rated at or above the fuse, so it outlasts the fuse.

Simplified classroom model: standard fuses 3 A, 5 A and 13 A; fuses and switches go on the live wire.

Quick check

A 2 kW heater at 220 V should use

I = 2000/220 ≈ 9.1 A. 3 A would blow; the lowest standard rating above 9.1 A is 13 A, so the cable must be rated ≥ 13 A. Fuses go on live, never neutral or earth.

Write it out

A 1.5 kW kettle at 220 V — choose the fuse and cable.

  1. Working current: \(I = P/V = 1500/220 \approx 6.8\ \text{A}\).
  2. Fuse: the lowest standard rating above 6.8 A is 13 A (5 A is too small).
  3. Cable: rated ≥ 13 A, so it outlasts the fuse.
