2Common mistakes

Force is not along v

The magnetic force is perpendicular to B and to the current or velocity.

✗F points along v and speeds the charge up.
✓F ⟂ v: direction changes, speed does not.

Electron flips the rule

The left-hand rule is drawn for conventional current, not for the electron.

✗Electron: force follows the rule as drawn.
✓Electron: I is opposite v, so F reverses.

Angle from the normal

For the coil, φ is measured from the coil normal, not from the plane.

✗Plane parallel to B: φ = 0, torque zero.
✓Plane parallel to B: φ = 90°, torque largest.

Half-turn reversal

The split ring swaps the coil current every half-turn, so the couple keeps one sense.

✗A coil spins steadily with no commutator.
✓Without it the coil stalls at φ = 0.

Quick check

Where is the force on the electron?

B. Conventional current is opposite v, so the rule gives force down; the same rule puts a positive charge up. F is perpendicular to B, ruling out C, and to v, ruling out D.

Write it

Write the torque relation and the angle it uses.

\(\tau = N B I A \sin\phi\), where \(\phi\) is between B and the coil normal. \(\tau = 0\) when the normal is along B, and largest when the coil plane is parallel to B (\(\phi = 90°\)).

The split-ring commutator reverses the coil current every half-turn, so the couple keeps one sense and the coil keeps turning: a d.c. motor.
