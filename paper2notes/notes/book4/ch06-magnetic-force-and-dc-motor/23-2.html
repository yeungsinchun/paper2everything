Section 23.2

Force on currents and charges

AForce on a straight wire

Key idea

Force magnitude \(F = B I l \sin\theta\), \(\theta\) between B and wire. Direction by Fleming's left-hand rule: First finger = Field (B), seCond = Current (I), thuMb = Motion/Force (F), all mutually perpendicular. Opposite for electron current vs conventional.

Key ideaDetail
Angle\(\theta=90°\) → max; \(0°\) → zero.
Parallel wiresSame direction attract; opposite repel (each wire's B acts on the other's I).

Quick check

Two parallel wires carry 3 A same direction, 5 cm apart. Force on 0.5 m length is approximately (μ0 factor)

B of one at other: μ0I/2πr≈12 μT; F=B I l≈18 μN, attractive for same.

BForce on a moving charge

Key idea

\(F = B Q v \sin\theta\) (θ between B and v). Direction follows LHR for + charge; for electron opposite (since charge negative). Perpendicular uniform B gives circular/helical motion (centripetal = BQv). Crossed E and B velocity selector: undeflected when \(qE = qvB\) → \(v = E/B\).

Key ideaDetail
WorkMagnetic force does no work (⟂ v) so speed stays, direction changes.
HallCharge separation builds Hall voltage balancing BQv.

Quick check

An electron enters uniform B out of the page with velocity to the right. Its deflection is

For a positive charge F = q(v × B) points down (v to the right crossed with B out of the page is down); an electron is negative so the force is opposite — up. Equivalently, the conventional current is opposite to the electron's velocity.
