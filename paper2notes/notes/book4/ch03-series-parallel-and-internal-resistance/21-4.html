Section 21.4

Internal resistance and meters

ATerminal voltage

Key idea

Real source: \(\varepsilon\) internal, \(r\) in series. Closed loop: \(\varepsilon = I(R+r)\), terminal \(V = I R = \varepsilon - I r\). Open circuit I=0 → V=ε. Short R→0 → I=ε/r. Measuring V vs I gives slope −r, intercept ε.

Key ideaDetail
GraphV vs I is straight line downwards; r = −slope.
PowerMax power to load when \(R = r\) (matching).

Quick check

A 6 V cell with r=1 Ω feeds a 2 Ω load. Current and terminal voltage are

Total 3 Ω → I=2 A. V=IR=4 V (or ε−Ir=4).

BMeters and earthing

Key idea

Ammeter in series should have ~0 resistance (otherwise lowers I). Voltmeter in parallel should have >>R (otherwise shunts current and reads low). Earthed point sets 0 V reference; any voltage is measured relative to earth.

Key ideaDetail
LoadingVoltmeter 1 kΩ across 10 kΩ divider reads noticeably low; 1 MΩ reads nearly true.
EarthChassis earthed for safety; touching live while earthed completes path.

Quick check

A voltmeter of 1 kΩ across a 10 kΩ resistor in a 10 V divider (another 10 kΩ) reads

~0.91 V. Equivalent 10k//1k≈0.91k, total ~10.91k, I≈0.92 mA, drop over lower ≈0.83 V? Actually ~0.9 V demonstrates loading. Ideal would be 5 V.
