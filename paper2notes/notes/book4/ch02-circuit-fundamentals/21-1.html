Section 21.1

Current, potential difference and e.m.f.

ACurrent as rate of flow

Key idea

Current \(I = \Delta Q/\Delta t\) (C/s = ampere). One coulomb is \(\sim 6.2\times10^{18}\) electrons. Drift velocity is slow (mm/s), but energy transfer is near light speed because the field propagates. Number of electrons \(n = Q/e\).

Key ideaDetail
ConventionCurrent arrow is motion of + charge. In a metal wire electrons drift opposite to the arrow.
Charge\(Q = I t\). Example: 2 A for 30 s → 60 C → \(3.7\times10^{20}\) electrons.

Quick check

A 2 A current passes for 1 minute. How much charge passes a cross-section?

Q=It=2×60=120 C. Option B forgets minutes→seconds.

Be.m.f. vs p.d.

Key idea

e.m.f. \(\varepsilon = E/Q\) through the source (chemical/mechanical → electrical). p.d. \(V = E/Q\) outside the source (electrical → heat/light). In an ideal loop, sum of p.d.s around = e.m.f. For a cell with internal r: \(\varepsilon = V + I r\).

Key ideaDetail
UnitsBoth J/C = volt. e.m.f. is not a force, just a name.
MeterVoltmeter across a component reads its p.d.; ideal draws no current.

Quick check

A cell has e.m.f. 1.5 V and internal resistance 0.5 Ω. When delivering 1 A to a load, terminal voltage is

V=ε−Ir=1.5−0.5=1.0 V. The missing 0.5 V drops inside the cell.
