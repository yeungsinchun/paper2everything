Section 21.2

Resistance, Ohm's law and resistivity

How current scales with voltage, and what sets a wire's resistance.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • R = V/I, I-V shapes, Ohm special case, resistivity ρ = RA/l, temperature effect

AI–V shapes

Key idea

Resistance defined as \(R = V/I\) (gradient inverse). Ohm's law is the special case where \(R\) is constant (I ∝ V straight line). Filament: R rises with temperature → curve flattens. Diode: near-zero for reverse, turns on after threshold.

Key ideaDetail
OhmicMetal at constant T: straight. Use R = slope inverse.
FilamentAs V ↑, T ↑ → R ↑ → curve bends toward V axis.
DiodeNon-ohmic, not linear; resistance not meaningful in one number.

Concept check

Which conductor obeys Ohm's law?

Only constant-T metal is linear. Filament and diodes are non-ohmic.

BResistivity and temperature

Key idea

For a uniform wire, \(R = \rho l/A\) with \(\rho\) resistivity (Ω m). \(\rho\) depends on material and T. Metals: ρ rises with T (lattice vibrations). Semiconductors: ρ falls sharply with T (more carriers freed).

Key ideaDetail
GeometryDouble l → double R; double A → half R. For same volume, stretching thin raises R fast (R ∝ l^2).
Ratio exampleTwo wires same length, radii r and 2r, same ρ: R1/R2 = (1/πr^2)/(1/π(2r)^2)=4.

Concept check

Parallel wires of same material and length, radii r and 2r, carry same total current. Ratio of current in thin to thick?

Same V so I ∝ 1/R ∝ A ∝ r^2. Thick has 4× area → 4× current, so thin:thick =1:4. Same total I splits 1/5 vs 4/5.
