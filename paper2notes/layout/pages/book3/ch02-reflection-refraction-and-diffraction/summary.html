Chapter 2 summary

Reflection, Refraction and Diffraction

Key ideas

  • Illustrating waves. Wavefronts join points in the same phase; rays show the direction of travel and meet wavefronts at right angles.
  • Reflection. At a boundary, the reflected wave obeys angle of incidence = angle of reflection, measured from the normal. Reflection can reverse a pulse at a fixed end.
  • Refraction. A wave changes direction when its speed changes across a boundary. Frequency stays fixed; wavelength changes because v = fλ.
  • Diffraction. Waves spread after passing an opening or edge. Spreading is strongest when the gap is comparable to the wavelength.

Formula sheet

v = fλ; i = r; n = c/v

v is wave speed, f frequency, λ wavelength; i and r are angles from the normal; n is refractive index.

Definitions to recall

Wavefront: line joining points in phase. Normal: perpendicular to a boundary. Diffraction: spreading at an opening or edge.
