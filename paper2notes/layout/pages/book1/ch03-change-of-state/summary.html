Book 1 · Chapter 3

Change of State · Summary

Review what changes in a phase transition, what stays fixed, and how to calculate transferred energy.

ADefinitions

Evaporation
Liquid to gas at the surface; can occur below the boiling point.
Boiling
Vaporization throughout a liquid at its boiling point.
Latent heat
Energy absorbed or released during a change of state.
Specific latent heat
Latent heat per unit mass, measured in J kg⁻¹.
Internal energy
Total molecular kinetic and potential energy.

BFormulae

\[E=m\ell\]

Use \(m\) for the mass that changes state. Use \(\ell_f\) for fusion and \(\ell_v\) for vaporization.

\[\ell_f(\text{ice})=334\,\mathrm{kJ\,kg^{-1}},\qquad \ell_v(\text{water})=2260\,\mathrm{kJ\,kg^{-1}}\]

For a process with temperature changes and phase changes, add every \(mc\Delta T\) and \(m\ell\) stage.

CEnergy and molecular model

Process Energy transfer Temperature Average molecular PE
Melting, vaporization into substance constant during phase change increases
Freezing, condensation out of substance constant during phase change decreases
Heating/cooling within one phase into/out of substance changes approximately unchanged

Temperature is linked to average molecular kinetic energy. A flat heating or cooling curve does not mean that energy transfer stops.

DCommon traps

  • Visible white mist above hot water is tiny liquid droplets, not invisible water vapour.
  • Evaporation is not restricted to boiling temperature.
  • At a phase-change plateau, energy changes molecular potential energy while average kinetic energy stays constant.
  • Do not use the whole apparatus mass in \(E=m\ell\); use only the mass transformed.
  • Convert masses to kilograms and energy values to matching units before calculating.
