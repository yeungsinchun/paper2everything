Forces and Newton I

No net force, no change in motion.

Goals

Students should be able to:

  • describe the meaning of inertia and its relationship to mass

  • state Newton’s First Law and use it to explain rest or uniform motion

  • understand friction as a force opposing motion/tendency of motion

  • find the vector sum of coplanar forces graphically and algebraically

  • resolve a force into components along two perpendicular directions

AAdding and splitting forces

Net force

\( \vec F_{\text{net}} = \sum \vec F_i \) (vector sum). Graphical: triangle/parallelogram; algebraic: add \( x \) and \( y \) components separately. A zero net force is not zero motion — it is zero acceleration.

Quick check

Forces \( 1\ \text{N} \) and \( 3\ \text{N} \) left, \( 5\ \text{N} \) and \( 2\ \text{N} \) right (right = +). Net?

Left is negative: \( -1-3=-4\ \text{N} \); right is positive: \( +5+2=+7\ \text{N} \). \( F_{\text{net}}=-4+7=+3\ \text{N} \), i.e. 3 N to the right.

BInertia and friction

Newton I

If \( \vec F_{\text{net}}=0 \) then \( \vec v \) does not change (at rest stays at rest, moving stays uniform). Inertia measures resistance to change — larger mass means larger inertia. Friction never helps motion; it opposes it.

Quick check

Which explains stopping distance after seeing danger?

C — during reaction time you still move at speed \( v \) (Newton I), then kinetic friction does \( F=ma \) to decelerate.

CResolving into components

Resolution

\[ F_x = F\cos\theta \qquad F_y = F\sin\theta \] Choose axes that make the add easy — often one along the slope.

Quick check

A force \( 10\ \text{N} \) at \( 30^\circ \) to the horizontal. \( F_x \)?

A — \( 10\cos30=8.66 \), \( F_y=5 \).

DStatic vs kinetic friction

Quick check

Friction always opposes the motion, not causes it.

True — it opposes sliding/tendency; it can’t initiate motion alone.

QDSE practice

2012 MC Q5

Two forces of fixed magnitude act at one point. How does their resultant change as the angle between them increases from 0° to 180°?

It decreases from F₁ + F₂ to |F₁ − F₂|, since R² = F₁² + F₂² + 2F₁F₂ cos θ.
